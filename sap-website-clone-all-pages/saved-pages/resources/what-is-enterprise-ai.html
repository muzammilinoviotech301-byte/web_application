What is enterprise AI?

Enterprise AI is the use of artificial intelligence (AI) to make business and manufacturing processes less manual, time-consuming, and prone to human error. Using enterprise AI platforms, many businesses across all industries are beginning to adopt AI at scale.

Why has enterprise AI become so popular?

Just like with the development of the internet in the 1990s and cloud computing in the 2010s, many businesses have approached AI with caution: Would it live up to the buzz? Or would it just be a fad that wouldn’t add real value to business and industry?

For many early adopters of enterprise AI, the results have shown that it creates a tangible competitive advantage. These businesses have seen significant improvements and efficiency gains across a spectrum of AI use cases, from data analysis, forecasting, and decision-making, to process automation, workplace productivity, and product development. Types of enterprise AI

One of the most interesting aspects of enterprise AI is that it can be used to perform both routine and transformational tasks. Examples:

  • Routine: An employee saves a few minutes (and some frustration) by using an AI copilot to find a document that’s been stored in the wrong place. Routine use cases may sound small, but they can really boost productivity at scale.
  • Transformational: A manufacturer saves millions of dollars by using deep learning to predict when its machines will need to have parts replaced—before they break.

This extreme diversity in AI use cases is impressive but can also make it hard to decide where to begin with AI adoption. A good place to start is by becoming familiar with the main types of business AI and their common applications.

Main types of enterprise AI

Type

Description

Common Uses

Enterprise machine learning

Foundational technologies designed to analyze large volumes of data to identify patterns, make predictions, and learn from outcomes. Includes deep learning for advanced speech recognition and computer vision.

Retail personalization, predictive maintenance, financial risk management, supply chain and logistics optimization, crop yield prediction, and disease detection.

AI copilots and assistants

Applications that act as personal assistants for employees. They adapt and improve over time by learning from user interactions.

Automating administrative tasks, transcribing meetings, accelerating coding, identifying security and quality issues, and aiding in resource location across extensive networks.

Generative AI

Tools that use human-like logic and language to assist in the creation of new content and data models. Capable of generating marketing content, designing products.

Creating sales and marketing content, designing new products, generating realistic synthetic data for AI model training, and supporting rapid prototyping and innovation.

Natural language processing (NLP)

Underlying AI capability that understands, interprets, and generates human language.

Enabling chatbots and virtual assistants to interact with humans to automate routine customer support, allowing human workers to spend more time on higher-value interactions.

Robotic process automation (RPA)

Applications that perform tasks that are routine, repetitive, and time-consuming for humans, integrating across different software systems to manage complex workflows.

Enhancing ERP and CRM systems by streamlining operations across sales and customer service, HR, and analytics by handling data entry, processing transactions, automating other digital tasks.

Enterprise AI benefits

As AI tools for business like copilots, chatbots, and generative AI become more sophisticated and widely adopted, organizations using these technologies are constantly discovering innovative new ways to use them. Before we explore more specific AI use cases, let’s look at some of the high-level benefits of business AI.

Workplace experience

AI plays a crucial role in improving the workplace environment by supporting and augmenting the capabilities of employees:

  • Employee productivity tools: AI copilots allow employees to focus on higher-value work by helping manage emails, schedule meetings, and prioritize tasks.
  • Adaptive learning systems: AI-driven learning platforms provide personalized training and development, adapting to the learning preferences and speeds of individual employees, making professional growth more engaging and effective.
  • Workplace analytics: AI tools empower teams to enhance employee satisfaction and performance by analyzing work patterns and providing suggestions for optimizing workflows.

Strategic decision-making

The integration of AI into decision-making processes helps businesses enhance accuracy and timeliness:

  • Data-driven insights: AI copilots and other enterprise machine learning tools help teams make informed decisions swiftly by analyzing extensive datasets and providing strategic recommendations.
  • Enhanced forecasting: Enterprise generative AI tools equip businesses to better anticipate market demands and consumer behavior by using past data to predict future trends.
  • Scenario analysis: Advanced simulation models help businesses explore various strategic scenarios, enabling effective risk management and planning.

Customer experience

AI transforms how businesses interact with their customers by providing personalized and responsive services:

  • Personalization at scale: AI analyzes customer data to tailor marketing messages and product recommendations.
  • 24x7 customer support: Chatbots provide instant responses to customer inquiries at any time, improving service availability and customer satisfaction.
  • Customer insights: By collecting and analyzing customer feedback and behavior, AI helps businesses understand and anticipate customer needs, leading to better service and product offerings.

Enterprise AI challenges

In addition to the benefits, there are some common challenges that businesses interested in becoming AI enterprises should plan for. These are primarily cultural resistance, ethical concerns, and technical barriers. Effective navigation of these issues is crucial for the long-term success of enterprise AI adoption.

Cultural and organizational hurdles

  • Challenge: While some employees may be enthusiastic about adopting AI because they see it as a tool to enhance their work, others may see it as a threat to their jobs.
  • Best practices: Having transparent discussions about AI’s benefits and its role in the organization can help align employee perspectives with strategic goals. Be understanding and compassionate if employees express concerns. Offering training programs can also help employees gain confidence with new AI tools.

Ethical and security considerations

  • Challenge: AI introduces ethical and security considerations that require special guidelines and security protocols.
  • Best practices: Developing responsible AI guidelines helps ensure everyone in the organization uses AI safely and fairly. Also, implementing advanced security measures helps protect sensitive data.

Integration complexity

  • Challenge: Successfully integrating AI into existing systems requires strategic planning and careful implementation.
  • Best practices: Beginning with pilot projects allows for gradual integration and helps identify and address potential challenges in a controlled environment.

Generative AI: enterprise AI for ERP and CRM

One of the most exciting and broadly applicable types of enterprise AI is copilots for ERP systems, which are powered by generative AI. AI copilots infuse companies’ existing ERP and CRM systems with natural language AI capabilities, which can dramatically enhance how employees operate and innovate by making processes more adaptive, intelligent, and optimized—usually without requiring major overhauling of existing systems.

Innovative applications and strategic impact

Improved user experience and productivity

Generative AI enables natural language interactions with ERP systems, streamlining processes and boosting productivity. For instance, large language models can simplify compliance by interpreting legal texts and identifying relevant criteria. This extends to CRM, where AI enhances sales and service interactions by automating responses and personalizing communication based on customer data insights.

Automation of manual tasks

In addition to chat applications, enterprise generative AI tools can be used to reduce manual labor in processes like supply chain logistics and customer data management. For example, AI can automate the digitization of delivery notes and customer interaction records, drastically cutting down costs and processing times.

Business process optimization

By analyzing data generated by business systems, enterprise generative AI tools identify patterns to optimize processes across ERPs and CRMs. They can generate ready-to-use process models and insights, and help businesses to implement best practices.

Analytics and decision-making

AI democratizes data-driven decision-making by making it easier for leaders and teams alike to work with complex analytics.

ERP and CRM cloud integration

AI capabilities for ERP and CRM systems are typically cloud-based. Organizations that rely on on-premise or private cloud infrastructures may need to adopt a public cloud to implement AI solutions. Businesses wanting to keep IT on premise can create a hybrid cloud environment by adding a public cloud to their existing infrastructure. Hybrid clouds allow businesses to maintain control over critical data while benefiting from advanced AI, cybersecurity, and scalability solutions offered by cloud providers.

Enterprise AI examples across industries

Beyond general-purpose business solutions, enterprise AI also includes industry-specific solutions.

Automotive

Automotive companies are using AI to enhance quality control and develop technologies for safe autonomous driving.

Energy

By forecasting demand precisely and adjusting supply, AI helps amplify the integration and value of renewable energy sources.

Entertainment

Streaming services are using AI to personalize viewer recommendations, and game developers and platforms are creating more responsive, realistic environments.

Finance

Advanced fraud detection systems and robo-advisors, powered by AI, significantly improve efficiency and security in the financial sector.

Healthcare

AI has had a dramatic impact in healthcare, improving the accuracy of diagnostics, allowing care providers to personalize medicine, and enhancing treatment outcomes through cognitive analysis of clinical data.

Life sciences

Companies in drug discovery and genomic research have seen reduced costs and time to market thanks to AI, and clinicians are now able to tailor medical treatments to individual genetic profiles.

Manufacturing

Many manufacturers are using enterprise AI to boost production efficiency, minimize downtime with predictive maintenance, and improve sustainability.

Public sector

AI is allowing organizations to automate administrative processes, optimize urban management systems for traffic and emergency services, and increase civic engagement.

Software development

Enterprise generative AI tools streamline coding and testing processes to accelerate development cycles, identify security vulnerabilities, and help developers learn new languages and frameworks faster.

Telecom

In the telecom industry, AI-powered predictive analytics enhance service delivery and network management, helping prevent outages and offering personalized customer experiences.

Enterprise AI strategy and planning

Becoming an AI enterprise involves more than just selecting the right platform or product—it also involves a dedicated AI strategy, smart planning, and teamwork. Businesses get the most value from their AI investments when they set clear objectives, encourage cross-team collaboration, and commit to ongoing learning. Here are some key considerations when getting started:

In-house vs. partner-led implementation

An important early step is deciding whether to plan and implement an enterprise AI platform or solution using in-house resources or to hire an expert partner.

In-house implementation

Implementing enterprise AI solutions without the help of an external partner can be cost-effective if the organization has the necessary technical expertise, resources, and bandwidth. This also offers advantages like total control over project execution and confidentiality of sensitive information. However, in-house implementations may pose challenges for some organizations, including a steep learning curve and substantial investment of employees’ time.

Partner implementation

While initially costly, hiring an enterprise AI partner provides businesses with specialized knowledge, resources, and experience. It allows organizations to confidently implement their solution knowing they’re following industry best practices. Working with a partner can also help mitigate skills gaps, and generally accelerates the process. Partner-led implementations require careful selection of vendors who align with the organization's objectives and culture.

Assessment and goal setting

It’s critical to assess existing capabilities and set clear goals:

  • Goal alignment: Define specific, measurable goals that the AI solution should meet. These goals should align with broader business objectives and have clear metrics for success. For instance, if reducing operational costs is a priority, set a target percentage reduction as a goal for AI initiatives.
  • Data audit: Perform a robust audit of data to evaluate quality, accessibility, and security. This assessment will be crucial to identify blockers and areas that need to be improved before the technical implementation begins.

Resource allocation

Assessing budgetary and human capabilities are vital to success:

  • Budgeting: Allocate adequate financial resources not just for the acquisition of enterprise AI technology but also for ongoing expenses like software updates, cloud storage, and cybersecurity measures.
  • Talent acquisition: Hiring skilled personnel or training existing employees to handle new AI tools is imperative. The technological investment will only work as planned if the people using it have the right skills.

Collaboration

Business AI projects are most successful when everyone benefiting from them participates:

  • Cross-functional teams: Form teams that include members from IT, data analytics, leadership, and end-users. This inclusive strategy helps ensure that all potential impacts and benefits are considered from the outset.
  • Regular reviews: Set up regular review sessions to evaluate the progress of AI integration against the project goals. These reviews can help realign efforts with business objectives and adapt to new challenges or opportunities.

Adaptation and learning

As enterprise AI continues evolves, businesses need to expect that their strategies and practices around using it need to evolve, too:

  • Continuous training: Develop ongoing training programs for employees to keep up with AI advancements helps staff stay informed and confident in their abilities.
  • Feedback mechanism: Establish mechanisms to collect feedback on AI implementations from all stakeholders. This feedback is invaluable for refining AI strategies and tools, helping to ensure they remain effective and aligned with user needs over time.

Conclusion: How enterprise AI is redefining business

Enterprise AI—including well-established types like machine learning and newer types like copilots and generative AI—are transforming businesses by enhancing their productivity, decision-making, and innovation. The positive results experienced by early adopters of business AI indicate that AI tools for business are not just a passing trend, but a necessity for remaining competitive in the digital world.

However, becoming a successful AI enterprise isn’t just about choosing the right enterprise AI platform. It’s also necessary to foster a workplace culture that thrives with AI. This involves training, data security investments, and using AI responsibly.
