Workforce management trends and evolutions

Workforce management (WFM) has undergone a significant transformation in recent years, driven by changes in how and where work gets done. Fortunately, workforce management software has evolved right along with it to help you better navigate this shifting landscape. Today’s WFM solutions include AI-enabled tools that help ensure the right employees and skillsets are available to meet demand. Modern WFM solutions must go beyond operational efficiency. They should support evolving employee lifestyles and expectations while promoting workforce well-being and engagement.

Workforce management definition

WFM is a comprehensive and integrated set of processes and tools used to accurately budget for, schedule, track, and forecast working times and workloads. WFM tasks include time and attendance, employee scheduling, and labor law compliance. By providing data-driven insights, WFM helps you better align your HR activities with your business goals—and quickly spot opportunities to streamline and boost productivity.

Modern WFM platforms leverage AI to automate scheduling, optimize staffing, and deliver predictive insights that help organizations stay agile and competitive.

Key components of workforce management

Workforce management software capabilities can range from the core functionalities of time management, scheduling, and compliance to more advanced integration and predictive analytics.

  • Time and attendance management: Top workforce management solutions should support time tracking from a multitude of sources, such as mobile applications and online timesheets. Self-service capabilities to request time off and grant approvals make life easier for both employees and managers, while alerts for overtime can help organizations avoid excessive payroll costs.
  • Forecasting, scheduling, and budgeting: Solutions that use predictive modeling, AI analytics, in-memory data processing, and other advanced technologies can create the most accurate forecasts and scheduling. With these solutions, scheduling can be automated based on workloads as well as factors like people’s skills, preferences, and availability.
  • Analytics dashboards and metrics: Based on real-time workforce data, analytics dashboards and metrics provide insights into employee performance, scheduling effectiveness, and more. When WFM systems can integrate with other HR and operational data, businesses can measure the impact of workforce strategies across the organization and tweak where needed.
  • Worker wellness, health, and safety: Having a good management and communication structure is essential to maintaining employee wellness. Today’s WMF solutions can also help you use real-time workplace data and personalized reporting to ensure you’re on top of any risks or challenging situations your teams may be facing.
  • Regulatory compliance: Configurable tools help companies track and comply with local and global laws, certification requirements, labor standards, union agreements, and more. With increasingly remote and geographically distributed teams, it’s essential to have a centralized view of regulations as they can vary greatly from region to region.
  • Mobile workforce management: Secure, anywhere access to workforce management data and processes helps field teams, remote workers, and on-site employees and managers stay connected and work more efficiently.
  • Integration: Integration with HR, procurement, and ERP systems can help you gain unprecedented insights. This means on-demand reporting and analysis, predictive planning for spikes and dips in hiring, and the ability to attach ROI to your cross-business workforce activities.

Top nine benefits of workforce management solutions

Using real-time data, AI, and cloud-based workforce management solutions, you can gain a competitive edge. With strong communication and planning strategies, you can optimize these WFM solutions by ensuring your management protocols and values are evenly applied across your business.

An engaged, happy, and motivated workforce is the core benefit from which all other WFM benefits can arise, including:

  1. AI-enabled analytics: Forecasts demand, identify workforce trends, and support data-driven decision making
  2. Automated attendance tracking: Streamlines the process of monitoring employee hours, reducing errors and administrative workload
  3. Lower costs: Optimizes staffing levels and schedules, minimizing overstaffing and reducing labor costs
  4. Improved workforce productivity: Aligns employee skills and schedules with business needs, enhancing workforce efficiency
  5. Compliance assurance: Helps ensure adherence to labor laws and regulations, reducing the risk of non-compliance penalties
  6. Increased employee satisfaction: Offers tools for self-service and flexible scheduling, improving employee engagement and retention
  7. Enhanced absence management: Centralizes leave requests and gives managers visibility into team availability
  8. Increased agility and resilience: Quickly scales talent and resources to respond to changing business needs
  9. Unified workforce data: Integrates employee and contingent worker information for better decision-making and strategic planning

Challenges of traditional workforce management solutions

Traditional workforce management solutions are increasingly strained under the weight of modern demands. Designed for more static, predictable environments, legacy systems often fall short in today’s digital-first landscape due to:

  • Limited agility and responsiveness
  • Fragmented workforce visibility
  • Inflexible scheduling and resource allocation
  • Lack of strategic insight
  • Risk exposure and compliance gaps
  • Inability to support skills-based transformation

Forbes highlights that mobility and flexibility are dismantling the traditional four walls of the enterprise, creating a “borderless workplace” where employees can work anytime, anywhere. Mobile solutions and unified device management enable secure access across all devices, while application-based interfaces and dynamic provisioning deliver a seamless, consumer-like experience. These changes fundamentally transform the relationship between employee and employer, and require modern IT infrastructures and agile cultures.

Organizations that proactively integrate AI into their WFM strategies are better positioned to build more adaptable, future-ready teams. This shift to automation, real-time responsiveness, and unified workforce visibility allows businesses to align their workforce to meet evolving demands and stay competitive.

What are the functions and features of WFM solutions?

Modern WFM solutions are designed to help organizations optimize labor resources, improve compliance, and enhance employee experiences.

Key WFM functions and features include:

  • Labor forecasting: Predicting where, when, what kind, and how many employees will be required to meet anticipated demand, enabling businesses to avoid overstaffing or understaffing and optimize productivity and labor costs
  • Scheduling: Aligning workforce availability, skills, and labor demand, ensuring the right people are assigned to the right tasks at the right time and helping organizations adapt in real time and engage employees
  • Time tracking: Recording employees’ time spent on tasks, projects, and work activities, enabling organizations to monitor productivity, manage attendance, ensure accurate payroll, and make informed decisions about resource allocation and project timelines
  • Absence management: Handling employee leave requests and tracking time away from work while ensuring compliance with labor regulations, maintaining workforce productivity, and supporting employee well-being
  • Analytics: Connecting data across HR and business systems to provide a complete view of the workforce, enabling organizations to make informed decisions, enhance employee experiences, reduce costs, and plan confidently for the future
  • Compliance: Consistently following legal, regulatory, and contractual standards related to employment to ensure fair treatment, reduce risk, and protect both employees and the business across jurisdictions
  • Mobile accessibility: Enabling the workforce to securely access and perform work using mobile devices, helping to ensure productivity, engagement, and a seamless, responsive experience across platforms, anytime, anywhere

What is external workforce management?

External workforce management is the strategic process of sourcing, engaging, and overseeing non-employee talent—also known as contingent workers—including freelancers, contractors, consultants, and service providers. Core functions of external workforce management include:

  • Sourcing and onboarding non-payroll talent
  • Managing services procurement
  • Ensuring compliance with labor laws
  • Controlling costs and optimizing spend
  • Gaining visibility into performance and outcomes

An external workforce is critical for modern organizations because it provides flexibility, scalability, and access to specialized skills that may not exist internally. The rise in remote work and a steadily growing dependence upon contingent workforces and services procurement has made external workforce management a priority in modern workforce strategies. Using contractors, freelancers, and service providers delivers strategic value by empowering companies to quickly adapt to changing market demands, control costs, accelerate innovation, and maintain business continuity. This flexibility is even more essential during periods of growth, transformation, or disruption.

Keeping a strong focus on external workforce management is crucial for ensuring agility and resilience in a fast-paced market. Using modern vendor management systems (VMS) and integrated workforce management solutions helps you:

  • Efficiently manage the entire lifecycle of external talent
  • Ensure seamless, compliant engagements that align with your company’s goals
  • Boost flexibility and innovation, allowing rapid incorporation of new skills and ideas while ensuring legal compliance

External workforce management effectively enables organizations to stay agile, tap into niche expertise, and respond swiftly to evolving business needs while maintaining operational efficiency.

What is distributed workforce management?

Distributed workforce management supports employees who work outside of a centralized office, providing them with connectivity and communication, ensuring compliance with regional laws, and enabling consistent HR processes. These employees can work with fully remote, hybrid, or flexible arrangements, and possibly across time zones and locations.

Supporting remote and hybrid teams across geographies delivers multiple benefits, including:

  • Access to global talent: Tapping into a broader pool of skills and expertise, unconstrained by location
  • Increased productivity: Collaborating across time zones, enabling around-the-clock productivity and faster project turnaround
  • Cost efficiency: Reducing overhead costs related to office space, utilities, and relocation
  • Business continuity: Increasing resilience to disruptions like natural disasters or local crises
  • Employee satisfaction: Improving work-life balance, which can boost morale and retention, and reduce turnover
  • Scalability: Quickly scaling up or down based on project needs without the constraints of physical infrastructure

Similar to on-site employees, a distributed workforce needs access to corporate systems, real-time collaboration tools, and consistent employee experiences regardless of location. Supporting these needs requires technology such as:

  • Cloud-based human capital management (HCM)
  • AI-enabled tools
  • Mobile accessibility

Driven by the demand for flexibility and the evolution of digital collaboration tools, many organizations prioritize distributed workforce management for operational continuity and to remain competitive in a digitally connected world.

What is total workforce management?

Total workforce management is an integrated approach to achieving more seamless management of both permanent and contingent staff, including freelancers, contractors, and gig workers. It emphasizes the importance of HCM integration with a VMS to ensure centralized visibility.

Collaboration between HR and procurement is essential for effective management of the entire workforce to meet business needs and support a shifting future. Total workforce management helps ensure that, regardless of the type of worker or nature of their employment, your company delivers consistent experiences and is fully compliant and fair in all hiring and management.

What is mobile workforce management?

Mobile workforce management refers to an organization’s use of digital tools—including mobile technology like smartphones and tablets—and applications to manage employees who work outside of a traditional office setting. This includes field technicians, delivery teams, or remote staff.

Businesses can improve productivity, ensure compliance, and deliver better service in real time with mobile access to scheduling, time tracking, task updates, and communication.

How to choose a workforce management solution

Workforce management software can come as a standalone program or as part of a broader HR system, such as a human resource information system (HRIS) with data management capabilities. Or it can fit into a complete human resource management system (HRMS) that unifies all human resource processes and data, from core HR and payroll to talent management. The functions of workforce management solutions are further enhanced when organizations can integrate them into ERP and procurement systems.

The ideal WFM software should also:

  • Use cloud technology for enhanced accessibility and scalability
  • Include advanced analytics and AI capabilities for predictive insights and intelligent workforce planning
  • Easily integrate with a VMS for effective total talent management, ensuring seamless total workforce management

The future of workforce management

Businesses today face changing cultures, global uncertainty, and fluctuating customer demands. Workforce expectations are evolving as employees expect flexibility, personalization, and purpose-driven environments. Work models are also more dynamic with hybrid, remote, and distributed teams requiring more agile workforce planning. To thrive in the working world of tomorrow, businesses will rely more heavily on their HR teams to find efficient and profitable solutions for modern workforce challenges.

And HR tech is certainly providing many of the answers to jumpstart their organization’s digital transformation—from automation to strategic enablement. Tasked with driving innovation and creating strategic value, today’s HR teams increasingly embed AI into core HR functions for automation, personalization, and predictive analytics.

To streamline operations, personalize employee experiences, and build dynamic, skills-based workforces, organizations should consider adopting integrated workforce management technology that delivers:

  • Better alignment of talent with business goals
  • Enhanced employee experiences and engagement
  • Improved agility and resilience in workforce operations

As the desire for remote work continues, organizations that support distributed workforces and have the right technology and HR processes in place can offset a talent crunch. However, creating a meaningful and satisfying workplace for employees who can’t work remotely is just as important.

Integrated and intelligent workforce management systems offer the flexibility and insights needed to support the future of work as it evolves—and wherever it occurs—even in times of unprecedented change.

Workforce management FAQs

  • What is remote and external workforce management?

    Remote workforce management involves overseeing and coordinating employees who work from various locations outside the traditional office. External workforce management oversees and coordinates non-employees, including freelancers, contractors, consultants, and service providers. Both of these workforce management tasks use technology to maintain communication, productivity, and engagement.

  • What is contingent workforce management?

    Contingent workforce management refers to the strategies and practices for managing non-permanent workers such as freelancers, contractors, and consultants, ensuring their effective integration and utilization. It involves coordinating procurement, onboarding, compliance, performance tracking, and payment to ensure these external contributors align with organizational goals and workforce strategies.

  • How does WFM software integrate with other business systems?

    WFM software often integrates seamlessly with other business systems like a human resource information system (HRIS), payroll, ERP, and a vendor management system (VMS), allowing for streamlined data management and enhanced operational efficiency.

  • How does WFM software support decision-making in workforce planning?

    WFM software provides data-driven insights and predictive analytics, helping managers make informed decisions about staffing, scheduling, and resource allocation, aligned with business objectives.

  • What is workforce management vs. workforce planning?

    Workforce management (WFM) focuses on the day-to-day operations of managing employees, including scheduling, time tracking, and compliance.

    Workforce planning, on the other hand, is a strategic process that helps organizations forecast future talent needs and align workforce capabilities with long-term business goals.

    In short, WFM is tactical and real-time; workforce planning is strategic and long-term. Both are essential to human capital management (HCM) solutions.

  • Is workforce management part of HR or operations?

    WFM software provides data-driven insights and predictive analytics, helping managers make informed decisions about staffing, scheduling, and resource allocation, aligned with business objectives.

  • What role does data analytics play in workforce management?

    Data analytics plays a critical role in workforce management by providing real-time insights into labor trends, productivity, and compliance. With predictive analytics, organizations can forecast staffing needs, optimize schedules, and make more informed decisions that align workforce operations with business goals. These goals include reducing costs, improving employee engagement, and boosting operational efficiency.
