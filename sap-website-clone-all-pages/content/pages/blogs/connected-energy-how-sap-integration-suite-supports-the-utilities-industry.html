Connected energy: How SAP Integration Suite supports the utilities industry

For the utilities industry, integration is not a back-office concern. It is the enabling layer of the energy transition itself. And SAP Integration Suite is built to deliver it.

The numbers behind the energy transition are striking. Renewable capacity additions are on track to match the entire installed power capacity of the world's largest economies by 2030.¹ Global energy storage installations are projected to grow 15-fold between 2025 and 2035.² Approximately 100 million households will rely on rooftop solar by 2030.³ Electric vehicles are reshaping demand curves in ways grid operators are only beginning to plan for.

Behind every one of these milestones is an integration challenge. Smart meters need to talk to billing systems. DER (Distributed Energy Resources) assets need to talk to grid operations. Carbon data needs to talk to regulatory reporting frameworks. Customer portals need real-time consumption data. Supply chains need visibility into equipment availability and procurement constraints. And all of this needs to happen across a landscape of diverse systems—many decades old, many from different vendors—that were never designed to work together at this speed or scale.

For the utilities industry, integration is not a back-office concern. It is the enabling layer of the energy transition itself. And SAP Integration Suite is built to deliver it.

Connecting the smart grid in real time

Regulatory mandates across Europe and beyond are driving a massive rollout of smart meters, with the EU's REPowerEU plan targeting 42.5% renewable capacity by 2030 alongside ambitious goals for smart metering infrastructure, flexibility data reporting, and electromobility integration.⁴ At the same time, according to McKinsey's Global Energy Perspective, energy affordability and security have risen to become critical policy priorities alongside decarbonization—and supply chain constraints are adding short-term pressure to clean technology investment timelines.⁵

The operational consequence is a step-change in real-time data volume. Meter readings, grid events, DER alerts, and consumption anomalies all need to reach the right application—billing, analytics, customer portals, demand response systems, grid management platforms—reliably and without delay. Traditional middleware was not designed for this. SAP Integration Suite's Advanced Event Mesh processes these events at scale, routing them in real time to every downstream system that needs them. Belgian water utility Farys deployed this architecture to build a smart water platform on SAP Business AI Platform—cutting invoice generation lead time from weeks to a single day, giving customers near-real-time consumption visibility for the first time, and achieving a 30% cost reduction through the economies of scale the integrated platform enabled.⁶

A platform for the full utilities value chain

SAP Integration Suite is SAP's industry-leading integration platform—unifying AI agents, applications, and data across SAP and third-party systems. For utilities, it serves as the connective layer across the entire value chain: smart metering and energy data management, customer billing and revenue management, asset operations, supply chain, regulatory compliance, and sustainability reporting.

As utilities face mounting pressure to modernize—from smart meter rollouts and grid decarbonization to tightening regulatory deadlines—the integration layer is where transformation succeeds or stalls. Siloed systems, fragile point-to-point connections, and legacy middleware create the exact friction that delays billing automation, slows compliance reporting, and prevents smart infrastructure investments from delivering their intended value. SAP Integration Suite resolves this with built-in capabilities that utilities need at every layer of the business: API management to govern how systems and partners connect and exchange data securely; event-driven architecture to process real-time signals from meters, grid assets, and DERs without delay; and B2B connectivity to exchange data reliably with energy market participants, regulators, and supply chain partners—all under a single governance framework that provides visibility, audit control, and policy enforcement across every integration.

Modernizing legacy architecture without risk

Many utilities are simultaneously running legacy integration infrastructure and planning cloud migrations—a combination that creates compounding risk. Every point-to-point connection is a potential failure point. Every system change carries the risk of cascading breakage across interdependent integrations.

Severn Trent Water, one of the UK's largest water utilities serving over 4.6 million households, faced this challenge when migrating its customer management platform to Kraken, a cloud-based CRM used by leading energy and water retailers across the UK. Aging middleware could not reliably synchronize approximately 220,000 customer records across billing, metering, asset management, and financial systems. Working with TCS, Severn Trent rebuilt its integration architecture on SAP Integration Suite—combining Cloud Integration for modular, reusable flows, API Management for secure third-party connectivity, and Advanced Event Mesh for high-volume event processing. The result: 40% faster time to market for system changes, 90% customer data alignment, and near real-time billing and asset updates. Severn Trent was recognized with the SAP Innovation Awards 2026 Technology Pathfinder award for this work.⁷

Automating compliance across a complex regulatory landscape

The utilities regulatory environment is among the most demanding of any industry. Carbon accounting requirements, CBAM (Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism) obligations, EU market communication mandates, grid settlement regulations, and national reporting frameworks all require utilities to aggregate data from multiple systems, format it for multiple bodies, and maintain immutable audit trails. Doing this manually is slow, error-prone, and increasingly unsustainable as reporting obligations multiply.

SAP Integration Suite automates this process—pulling data from generation, transmission, and distribution systems and routing it to compliance reporting workflows in the formats regulators require. SAP's Green Ledger integration ensures that sustainability and carbon accounting data flows directly into financial reporting, making it accurate, timely, and audit-ready. For utilities operating under the EU's Fit for 55 framework—which targets a 55% reduction in greenhouse gas emissions by 2030—this kind of integrated compliance infrastructure is not optional.⁵

Enabling the distributed energy future

The IEA's World Energy Investment 2025 report projects that global clean energy investment will reach $2 trillion in 2025—the first time it has hit that milestone.¹ A significant share of that investment will flow into distributed energy infrastructure: rooftop solar, battery storage, EV charging, and the grid upgrades needed to manage bidirectional energy flows. Cumulative U.S. DER investments alone are projected to eclipse $110 billion between 2020 and 2026.⁸

Managing distributed energy resources requires real-time integration between DER management systems, grid operations, billing platforms, and customer-facing applications. SAP Integration Suite's event-driven architecture handles this at scale—processing high-volume DER events with guaranteed delivery, loose coupling, and fault tolerance, enabling utilities to launch demand response programs, dynamic tariffs, and prosumer services without the latency and fragility of legacy middleware.

The integration foundation for what comes next

The energy transition is accelerating. The utilities that are best positioned to respond are not those with the most advanced individual systems—they are those with the most capable integration layer connecting those systems together.

SAP Integration Suite provides that foundation: a unified platform that connects the systems, data, and partners utilities need to operate today, comply today, and transform for tomorrow.
