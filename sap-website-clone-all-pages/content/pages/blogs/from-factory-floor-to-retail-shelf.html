From factory floor to retail shelf: how SAP Integration Suite supports consumer packaged goods

A promotion goes live at a major grocery chain. The retailer's order management system generates a purchase order in EDIFACT (electronic data interchange for administration, commerce and transport). The CPG manufacturer's ERP runs SAP S/4HANA. The trade promotion platform is Salesforce. The demand planning tool is third-party. And the logistics provider has its own EDI (electronic data interchange) spec, updated without warning last quarter.  And somewhere in the middle sits a trading partner network that expects every message to arrive in the right format, at the right time, with a full audit trail.

In most CPG organizations, stitching this together falls on an integration team managing a patchwork of platforms built up over years of acquisitions and point-solution decisions. When one connection breaks during peak season, the cost shows up in stockouts, retailer deductions, and missed promotional windows—not in an IT ticket.

This isn't an edge case. It's the operating reality for enterprise architects across food and beverage, health and beauty, and household goods. The CPG industry runs on some of the most demanding B2B integration environments in enterprise software—high EDI volumes, strict retailer compliance requirements, partner onboarding at scale, and integration debt that compounds every year systems are added without a unifying platform underneath them.

SAP Integration Suite is an AI-assisted integration platform as a service (iPaaS) that connects applications, systems, trading partners, and data across the full CPG value chain. Its Trading Partner Management capability handles EDI onboarding, message translation, and partner lifecycle management—so when a logistics provider changes their spec, one team makes one change, not six. Below are four scenarios that illustrate how it works in practice—grounded in real solutions and customer outcomes.

Scenario 1: Automating retailer EDI onboarding and trading partner management

The scenario: A CPG manufacturer selling through 40+ retail, club, and food service partners manages a different EDI specification for each one. Onboarding a new trading partner means weeks of custom mapping work, manual testing, and IT involvement at every step. When a retailer updates their spec, the change ripples across integration flows maintained in different tools by different team members.

How SAP Integration Suite helps: Trading Partner Management—a capability within SAP Integration Suite—centralizes B2B relationship configuration: partner profiles, EDI message agreements, and communication settings managed from a single governed interface. Integration Advisor uses AI-assisted mapping to accelerate the design of message transformations between formats such as ANSI X12, EDIFACT, and proprietary variants. New trading partners are onboarded through a repeatable, governed process rather than a custom project each time.

The outcome in practice: Nu Skin Enterprises—a consumer products company operating across 54 markets with US$1.73 billion in revenue—consolidated four separate integration solutions with SAP Integration Suite. Integration development time dropped from several weeks to 1–2 days, and the company saved US$350,000 in subscription costs for replaced integration technology and adapters.

Our business environment is changing fast, requiring us to embrace new technologies if we're to take advantage of new marketing scenarios. With SAP Integration Suite, we know we're able to adapt and integrate with new platforms quickly to make the most of emerging opportunities.1

Enterprise Architect, Nu Skin Enterprises Inc.

Scenario 2: Connecting real-time sell-through data to supply chain planning

The scenario: A beverage manufacturer's demand planning team works from overnight batch data. By the time sell-through signals from retail partners reach SAP Integrated Business Planning, the promotional window is already hours old. Replenishment decisions lag. Out-of-stocks during key promotional periods are a recurring issue that shows up in retailer scorecards.

How SAP Integration Suite helps: Advanced Event Mesh—the platform's enterprise-grade event streaming capability—enables sell-through data from retail partners to flow as real-time business events into SAP Integrated Business Planning. When stock at a given location crosses a defined threshold, a replenishment signal fires to the manufacturing plant automatically. Nothing waits for a batch run or a human to route a message.

The SAP Business Accelerator Hub includes over 5,000 pre-built integration content for consumer goods and retail scenarios—so teams start from validated, SAP-delivered scenarios rather than building from scratch. Its Trading Partner Management capability handles EDI onboarding, message translation across EDIFACT, ANSI X12, and other industry standards, and partner lifecycle management at scale—so when a logistics provider changes their spec, one team makes one change, not six.

The outcome in practice: Zabka, the Polish convenience retail chain operating 11,000 stores, uses SAP Integration Suite to run real-time franchise communications and delivery tracking across its entire network—refreshing location and stock data every 4 seconds across all locations. That means store-level inventory visibility updated 21,600 times per day, per location, giving franchise operators and central supply teams the same picture of stock in near real-time. For a network of that scale, the alternative is manual reconciliation, delayed replenishment decisions, and the kind of shelf gaps that cost convenience retailers disproportionately during high-footfall periods. 2

Scenario 3: Consolidating a fragmented integration landscape

The scenario: A global consumer goods manufacturer has grown through acquisition and now manages integrations across six separate platforms—a legacy ESB, two iPaaS tools, proprietary middleware, and bespoke scripts that predate anyone currently on the team. Maintenance costs are high. Monitoring is inconsistent. When something breaks at 2am, no one has a single place to look.

How SAP Integration Suite helps: Rather than maintaining separate integration stacks for different business units or acquired companies, SAP Integration Suite provides a single platform for all integration patterns—application-to-application, B2B/EDI, API, and event-driven—with consistent monitoring, alerting, and SLA tracking across every flow. The platform's open connector library connects 160+ non-SAP applications without custom development, and pre-built integration packages accelerate migration from legacy platforms.

For organizations on SAP Process Orchestration, the Migration Assessment capability estimates migration scope and generates a migration roadmap—reducing the uncertainty that typically stalls consolidation projects.

The outcome in practice: CONA Services—the technology and shared-services arm for Coca-Cola's North American bottlers—deployed SAP Integration Suite to consolidate and streamline processes across its bottling ecosystem, achieving a 50% reduction in runtime costs while enabling standardized workflows and real-time data sharing across bottling partners.

With the new integration infrastructure, we're well positioned to respond quickly to changing market conditions and seize new opportunities. The improved data exchange with partners and customers will strengthen our competitive position and increase retailer and distributor satisfaction.3

CONA Services

Fonterra, the New Zealand-based global dairy cooperative, consolidated multiple integration platforms—including MuleSoft—onto SAP Integration Suite as its single integration foundation, cutting maintenance costs and operational complexity across its global operation in the process. 4

Scenario 4: Supporting hybrid estates and regulated data exchange

The scenario: A food and beverage manufacturer operates a mix of cloud ERP, on-premise legacy systems, and third-party SaaS platforms across 30 countries. In regulated markets, data residency requirements mean certain payroll, financial, or customer records cannot leave the country. In others, manufacturing execution systems on the plant floor run air-gapped or on isolated networks that cannot connect directly to cloud services for operational safety or latency reasons. The integration platform needs to span both worlds—cloud-native where the architecture allows it, on-premise where it must—without forcing a wholesale migration or maintaining two separate integration stacks.

How SAP Integration Suite helps: Edge Integration Cell—the platform's hybrid deployment option—runs integration flows within a customer-managed private landscape while design, monitoring, and governance remain centralized in the cloud. CPG manufacturers with data sovereignty requirements, factory-floor systems with limited connectivity, or on-premise ERP landscapes can run governed integrations locally without losing the visibility and control of a unified platform.

This is also relevant for food safety traceability and regulatory compliance: Edge Integration Cell enables the governed, auditable data exchange records that supply chain transparency requirements demand—whether for retailer audit obligations, regulatory submissions, or internal compliance programs.

What the platform delivers at scale

These scenarios reflect a consistent pattern: CPG operations generate high volumes of data across distributed systems—retailers, co-manufacturers, logistics providers, DTC platforms—and the competitive value of that data depends on how quickly it can be connected, acted on, and governed.

For CPG companies evaluating whether a platform can handle their integration volumes at peak—Black Friday, promotional surges, new market launches—the operating scale of SAP Integration Suite provides a reference point. The platform processes around 152 billion messages in the 12 months to August 2026 across customers in 144 countries and 28 industries, with 99.99% monthly availability and less than 14 minutes of downtime per month. It connects 160+ non-SAP applications through Open Connectors—covering the third-party trade promotion, demand planning, and logistics platforms common in CPG landscapes—and provides more than 5,000 prebuilt integrations through the SAP Business Accelerator Hub, including content purpose-built for consumer goods, retail, and supply chain scenarios.5

Enterprise architects in CPG organizations face a structural tension: the business moves at the speed of promotions, partner onboarding, and market launches, while integration decisions made under that pressure create technical debt that compounds for years. When speed is the mandate, getting the approach right the first time matters more, not less—and that is where Integration Assessment becomes the most important conversation before any build starts.

SAP Integration Suite's built-in Integration Assessment capability, powered by the SAP Integration Solution Advisory Methodology (ISA-M), gives architects a structured, proven methodology to match integration requirements to the right patterns—API-led, event-driven, B2B, file-based, or hybrid—before the first deployment decision is locked in. It turns "we need this connected by Q3" into a defensible architecture rather than an expedient shortcut, reducing rework mid-program and accelerating the path from design to production value.

For CPG companies already running SAP S/4HANA or moving to RISE with SAP or GROW with SAP, that foundation matters beyond the immediate project. The integration architecture put in place today determines how quickly AI-driven demand sensing can be activated tomorrow, how reliably legacy systems stay connected during cloud transformation, and how efficiently new direct-to-consumer capabilities can be added without disrupting existing retailer relationships. The platform underneath the value chain is not an IT decision—it is a commercial one.
