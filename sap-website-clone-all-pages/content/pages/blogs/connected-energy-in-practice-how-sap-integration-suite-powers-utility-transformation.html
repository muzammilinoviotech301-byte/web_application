Connected energy in practice: How SAP Integration Suite powers utility transformation

SAP Integration Suite closes gaps in Utilities industry. The energy transition is not an abstract strategic ambition. It is happening now, in operations centers and field teams and billing departments across every utility in the world. Smart meters are being rolled out by the millions. Distributed energy resources (DER) are multiplying faster than grid operators anticipated. Regulatory reporting obligations are growing in complexity every quarter. Customer expectations—shaped by digital experiences in every other part of their lives—are rising.

And at the center of every one of these changes is a data exchange that needs to happen reliably, in real time, between systems that were never designed to work together.

The following scenarios illustrate where that integration pressure shows up most acutely—and how SAP Integration Suite is helping utilities resolve it.

Scenario 1: The CRM migration that almost stalled

A utility serving millions of households decides to modernize its customer management platform. The destination is clear: a modern, cloud-based CRM with better self-service, real-time account visibility, and lower maintenance overhead. The project is scoped, resourced, and approved.

Then the integration reality sets in. Fifteen years of point-to-point connections between the CRM, billing system, metering platform, asset management, and financial applications have created a fragile web. The existing middleware cannot reliably synchronize hundreds of thousands of customer records across all of these systems simultaneously. Every test migration surfaces data inconsistencies. The project risks slipping— not because of the destination platform, but because of the integration layer holding everything together.

This is exactly the situation Severn Trent Water faced. One of the UK's largest water and waste utilities, serving over 4.6 million households, Severn Trent set out to migrate to Kraken CRM—a modern cloud platform used by leading energy and water retailers across the UK. Its existing middleware could not support the migration reliably. Synchronizing approximately 220,000 customer records across Kraken, SAP ECC, Temetra metering, ArcGIS, and other core systems was beyond what the legacy architecture could handle.

Working with Tata Consultancy Services, Severn Trent rebuilt its integration architecture on SAP Integration Suite—combining Cloud Integration for modular, reusable flows with robust error handling, API Management for secure third-party connectivity, and Advanced Event Mesh for high-volume, event-driven processing. The new platform replaced fragile point-to-point connections with a governed, scalable architecture that could grow with the business.

The outcomes: 40% faster time to market for system changes, 90% customer data alignment across all 220,000 records, and billing and asset updates that now happen the same day rather than days later. Hundreds of IT hours previously consumed by manual reconciliation were recovered. The platform runs 24x7 with a smaller infrastructure and energy footprint than the legacy it replaced. Severn Trent received the SAP Innovation Awards 2026 Technology Pathfinder award for this work.¹

The TCS Integration team have successfully implemented a new strategic integration platform within Severn Trent, using SAP Integration Suite to control message flow between Kraken and SAP to ensure the alignment of customer and financial data across the systems.

— Greg Hammond, Portfolio Manager, Severn Trent

Scenario 2: Smart meters generating data no one can use

A water utility completes a major smart meter rollout. Every meter is transmitting readings—consumption data, pressure events, anomaly alerts—around the clock. The infrastructure investment was significant. The business case was built on real-time customer insights, faster billing, and predictive leak detection.

Six months in, billing still runs on monthly cycles. Leak alerts are processed manually. The data is arriving—but without an integration layer capable of routing it in real time to the right downstream systems, it is sitting in queues. The smart meters are working. The value is not.

Farys a Belgian water utility with €454 million in annual revenue, built the platform that resolves this scenario. Using SAP Integration Suite's Advanced Event Mesh, Farys processes smart meter readings and events in real time—making them immediately available across billing, analytics, customer portals, and predictive maintenance systems as they arrive. The architecture was built on SAP Business AI Platform, with SAP Cloud for Energy handling data collection, validation, and analysis.

The business impact was transformative: invoice generation lead time dropped from weeks to a single day. Customers gained near-real-time visibility into their own consumption for the first time. The platform created the foundation for AI-driven forecasting and predictive maintenance—capabilities that depend entirely on having clean, timely, integrated data. Farys also achieved a 30% cost reduction through the economies of scale the unified platform enabled.²

With SAP Business Technology Platform (now as BAIP), everything is integrated. Our data is in one place. It unlocks intelligence, simplifies innovation, and lowers our total cost of ownership compared to our peers.

— Inge Opreel, CIO, Farys

Scenario 3: Compliance reporting across multiple regulatory regimes

A transmission grid operator submits data to three separate regulatory bodies—each with different formats, retention requirements, and submission timelines. Today, a team of analysts extracts data manually from four systems, reconciles discrepancies, and formats reports. A single submission cycle takes two weeks. Any change in regulatory requirements triggers weeks of rework.

As decarbonization obligations intensify, this model is unsustainable. The EU's Fit for 55 framework targets a 55% reduction in greenhouse gas emissions by 2030.³ CBAM obligations are expanding. Market communication requirements are tightening across Europe. According to the IEA's World Energy Investment 2025 report, global clean energy investment reached $2 trillion in 2025—and the regulatory reporting infrastructure required to govern it is growing at a commensurate pace.⁴

SAP Integration Suite addresses this by automating data aggregation across generation, transmission, and distribution systems and routing it to compliance workflows in the formats each regulator requires. SAP's Green Ledger integration connects sustainability data directly to financial reporting, ensuring carbon accounting is accurate, auditable, and timely. The result is a compliance posture that scales with regulatory change—not one that requires manual rework every time an obligation shifts.

The pattern across every scenario

What the Severn Trent migration, the Farys smart metering platform, and the compliance automation scenario share is the same root cause: systems that hold valuable data, separated from the applications that need it by integration gaps that manual processes and legacy middleware cannot close at the speed the business requires.

SAP Integration Suite closes those gaps. Cloud Integration, API Management, Advanced Event Mesh, and B2B connectivity—combined with prebuilt utilities industry content covering meter lifecycle management, energy settlement, and market communication— give utilities the integration foundation to act on their data, meet their obligations, and deliver on the promise of the energy transition.

The technology exists. The customer outcomes are proven. The question for every utility is how long it can afford to wait.
