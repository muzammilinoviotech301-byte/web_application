Why a fractured customer journey isn’t a bad thing: The hybrid AI buyer enters the chat

New research by SAP Customer Experience underscores how much the customer journey has changed in the era of AI, and details what brands can do to meet the moment.

Brands who still believe they own the customer journey may want to sit down for this. Customers have chosen a new route to purchase, leaving a wide berth between the journeys of yesterday where brands controlled the outcomes and the path that’s taken today. And the divergence is especially visible when it comes to B2B.

The carefully crafted brand journey where discovery and evaluation happen on your own channels like your website, store, or sales team has gone by the wayside. Today discovery and evaluation increasingly take place inside an AI, well before a customer visits your site or speaks with someone on your team – if they reach out to your brand at all. When the customer shortlist is made by AI, either you’re on it, or you’re not. The true test of CX leaders now is understanding this new reality and acting on it.

The customer journey has split: Meet the hybrid AI buyer

SAP's AI in Customer Experience Report 2027 surveyed 4,800 consumers, business buyers, CX practitioners, and decision-makers in the United States and Germany. The research highlights the structural shift underlying the change in buyer behaviour.

The customer journey has split. The first half: discovering options, researching alternatives, comparing and shortlisting is increasingly happening inside AI tools before a customer ever interacts with a brand channel. The operational half: the purchase, the fulfilment, the service, the return all still fall to the business.

Today the customer journey is broken into two parts:

  1. The first half: This initial stage is increasingly happening inside an AI tool before a customer ever interacts with a brand channel, and includes discovering options, researching alternatives, comparing benefits and pricing and shortlisting.
  2. The second half: The next portion of the journey is considered the operational half because all of the elements within it, including the purchase, fulfilment, service and post-service, and returns all fall within the responsibilities of the business.

Nearly all business buyers surveyed noted that they’ve used AI for vendor selection or purchasing. While AI is leading the discovery and evaluation part of the journey, it transitions to a supportive role during the later stages of the process as business context, process, and interpersonal relationships take priority. After all, completing a B2B transaction still requires navigating negotiated prices, contract terms, approvals, and implementation plans. The key takeaway here: AI can identify the right supplier in moments. Doing business with the supplier still requires more time and a human touch.

This is the hybrid AI buyer: An experience that’s neither fully AI nor human-directed, but rather a cojoining of the two – and this hybrid buyer expects that businesses will be prepared to answer their questions or address their needs no matter which side they’re navigating from, but most brands aren’t prepared yet.

Half of U.S. consumers now use AI in at least some of their purchase journeys

Businesses have spent decades and big dollars building awareness, honing UX and digital experiences, and planning carefully curated paths to purchase. AI has flipped the script. The question is no longer, “Will customers find us?” It’s, “Can AI find us, understand us, and confidently recommend us?”

With half of our respondents in the U.S. now using AI in at least some of their purchase journeys, SAP Customer Experience Chief Product Officer Balaji Balasubramanian framed the issue: if your offerings are not known to AI engines, they won’t surface them at all. That is a discoverability problem.

And the issue is more than a visibility challenge. Discoverability no longer means conducting a quarterly catalog audit or SEO exercise. It’s now a moving target because AI models are continually deciding what to surface, compare, and recommend.

Each aspect of the journey: every image, specification, attribute, availability signal, and proof point has a direct impact on whether a product or service is discovered, shortlisted, or bypassed – by both humans and machines.

Today catalogs should be continuously improved by using AI signals generated from real customer behavior. For instance, search patterns can reveal a demand that hasn’t been met, while taxonomy analysis can demonstrate where products fall out of discovery. Content scoring can pull back the curtain on where descriptions and translations are missing the mark for your products.

SAP's Merchandising Assistant embodies this logic. Working across shop fronts, agentic search environments, and SEO at scale, it surfaces content issues, category misalignment, and assortment gaps, then drives the enrichment needed to fix them. The Catalog Optimization Agent continuously reviews product content against quality standards, identifying inconsistencies across languages and channels, and delivering actionable recommendations before problems become lost revenue.

The ability to be discovered in an AI-driven era is earned and re-earned continuously. Brands that realize this will be found AND suggested. To meet the demands of AI as a customer, businesses must use AI to optimize as a supplier.

Your brand has been found. What you do next determines if you’ll remain chosen.

Despite investment in AI-powered discovery, customers decide if the brand is relevant to them during the second half of the journey. In other words, experience begins where AI ends.

According to our research, 87% of U.S. consumers still want to review and approve purchases themselves. The hybrid journey isn’t a transitional state; it’s the preferred state – and brands must adapt.

The moments that follow discovery aren’t exactly shining moments for brands. More than six in ten consumers say AI falls short on customer service, returns, and exchanges. One in four respondents rate AI as very helpful when something goes wrong. These are the most critical moments of a customer relationship, and they fall entirely within the brand’s control.

While discovery has been disrupted by AI, the components that forge lasting loyalty remain the same. Customers remember how, or if, a problem was resolved. And they still tell people – and AI – about the experience they had.

To thrive in a hybrid AI world, brands will be both discoverable and worth discovering. AI may make the introduction, but every delivery, service interaction, and return is either a promise made or a promise broken. AI has taken the part of the journey that commoditizes brands. What’s left for brands is the part that differentiates them.

Reality check: 93% of CX decision makers say they’re prepared for AI. Only 6% report end-to-end system connection

AI isn’t a placebo to areas of your business that have been disconnected. It actually exposes and accelerates the disarray caused by those systemic gaps.

There’s a common thread woven throughout the business difficulties experienced amid the hybrid journey: the enterprise behind the customer experience isn’t connected enough to support AI at any point in the journey, let alone all of them.

93% of U.S. CX decision-makers say they feel prepared for AI-enabled customers. Only 6% report that all relevant systems are actually connected end-to-end.

Let’s take a retailer managing perishable goods as an example. Traditionally, once stock approaches an expiration date, markdowns are triggered. By nature, these are often unplanned, deep discounts that negatively impact margins. These discounts occur because the business is responding to a physical reality that their systems never converted into commercial foresight. Inventory data and promotion planning have been living in separate worlds and operating on different timescales.

In an autonomous enterprise, those worlds merge. AI doesn’t have to wait for a manager to identify a problem on a shelf walk because it reads expiration and inventory signals from ERP in real time, as well as demand patterns, promotion history, and customer segment behavior. AI can identify risk days in advance and create a targeted promotion to move stock to the right customer, at the right moment with a minimum viable discount.

The result isn’t just smarter markdowns; it’s fewer markdowns altogether.

This is the Autonomous Enterprise in action. AI sees both the customer-facing layer and the full operational layer beneath: demand sensing, inventory-aligned commercial decisions, catalog improvements based on real customer search signals. All of this feeds into a coherent strategy, rather than a cascade of disconnected reactions.

Traversing the hybrid journey

Brand used to be about persuasion, building awareness, creating preferences, and getting chosen. In an AI-mediated world, the moments of brand connection happen with check-out, order fulfilment, returns, and service. These are the moments where a brand can stand out, and they matter more than ever. AI decides whether your business should be recommended, and the experience determines if a customer will come back.

The journey has split, but the responsibility for both parts of the journey has never been more unified.
