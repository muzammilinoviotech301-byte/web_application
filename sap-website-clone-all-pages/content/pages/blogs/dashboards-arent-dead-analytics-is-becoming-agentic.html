Dashboards aren’t dead. Analytics is becoming Agentic

The next era of enterprise analytics will not be defined by the disappearance of dashboards. It will be defined by what happens after we look at them.

Thierry AudasPublished on November 11, 2026

Reports and dashboards remain indispensable. They provide a shared view of performance for decision-makers, trusted numbers for finance teams, and governed insights at scale and low cost. The next competitive advantage will come from shortening the distance between insights and actions.

Consider a sales leader who sees that margin in Europe has declined 4.2%. The dashboard has done its job. It establishes a trusted view of what happened. But this sales leader’s real work is just beginning. Why did margin fall? Which customers, products, and markets explain the change? Was it pricing, volume, product mix, cost, or a combination? Is the decline temporary or structural? Which actions could reverse it, and with what consequences?

Answering those questions requires exploration, analysis, hypothesis testing, and interpretation, a process that can take days. Agentic analytics compresses that cycle. Instead of simply consuming analytics, people can delegate analytical work to AI agents that investigate, reason, recommend, and act within governed context and boundaries.

This is not about replacing every report and dashboard with an agent. Generative AI is already changing how analytics experiences are delivered. Strategic planning assumptions from Gartner® indicate that “by 2028, 60% of existing dashboards will be replaced by genAI-powered narrative and visualization.”1

The important shift is not from reports and dashboards to agents. It is from analytics that primarily presents information to analytics that can increasingly participate in turning information into decisions and actions.

From analytical consumption to governed delegation

Enterprise analytics has steadily reduced the friction between people and insights. Dashboards have helped more people see data. Self-service analytics have allowed them to explore it. Conversational analytics allows them to ask questions in natural language. Agentic analytics introduces a fundamentally different step: delegation.

Instead of asking “What was European margin last quarter?”, a business leader could set an objective: investigate the decline, identify the root causes, and recommend a course of action. An agent can break that objective into tasks, identify relevant data, test hypotheses, invoke analytical tools, evaluate alternatives and explain its recommendations. That is not simply natural-language querying. It is analytical reasoning and orchestration.

Delegation does not mean full autonomy. It means giving an agent the appropriate latitude for each decision. A low-risk operational variance may require limited oversight. A financially material recommendation requires stronger validation. A regulated decision may require explicit human validation.

The disciplines that made enterprise analytics trustworthy, including governance, lineage, and security, become more important, not less. They must now extend to the agent itself: what it can access, which tools it can invoke, when it must escalate, and whether its reasoning can be traced.

For analytics leaders, the strategic question is not simply which analytical work to delegate, but under what controls. Corporate KPIs will continue to be well served by reports and dashboards. Statutory reporting requires deterministic output. Investigation, simulation, and recommendations may benefit from agentic support. The objective is the right analytical experience and level of autonomy for each decision, grounded in consistent business meaning.

The future is not autonomy without oversight. It is governed delegation: clear boundaries for agents, and accountability for the decisions and actions that follow.

The breakthrough isn’t the agent. It’s the context.

Large language models understand language remarkably well. They do not inherently understand your business. Ask an agent why margin is falling and it must know what your organization means by margin. Ask about revenue and it must know which measure is authoritative. Ask about Europe and it needs the relevant organizational hierarchy. Investigating a purchase order problem may require understanding how a customer relates to purchase orders, contracts, products, suppliers, inventory, and fulfillment.

Enterprise AI therefore needs more than access to data. Governed metrics remain essential, but agents also need context about business entities, relationships, policies, provenance, and processes. According to the 2026 Gartner Rethinking Analytics and AI Through Agentic Transformation Survey, “80% of D&A leaders agree that grounding AI agents in shared business context improves the accuracy and reliability of their outputs.”2 The implication for enterprise AI is clear: the quality of context is a more durable differentiator than the power of the model.

From trusted business context to trusted agentic analytics

If business context is foundational to agentic analytics, the strategic question becomes: where does that context come from, and how do we make it consistently available to people and AI?

This is where SAP has a distinctive opportunity. For many enterprises, SAP systems do more than hold data. They run the processes that create it. A customer connects to a purchase order. An order contains products. Products depend on inventory and suppliers. Fulfillment affects revenue. Revenue becomes a financial posting. For agentic analytics, those relationships matter as much as the data itself.

SAP Business Data Cloud brings together SAP and third-party data, data products, and analytics while preserving the business semantics that give data meaning across data sharing, applications, analytics, and AI scenarios.

SAP Knowledge Graph adds another critical dimension: relationships among business entities. By connecting business data with machine-readable context, it can help ground AI in the meaning and relationships behind enterprise data rather than requiring a language model to reconstruct that meaning probabilistically.

Together, SAP Business Data Cloud and SAP Knowledge Graph are foundational components of SAP Business AI Platform. The platform enriches the governance needed to scale agentic AI responsibly, with controls for access, guardrails, monitoring, traceability and human oversight, helping ensure that agents remain secure, compliant, and accountable as organizations delegate more work to AI.

This creates a larger strategic opportunity: what if the governed and trusted business context used to understand enterprise performance could become a common foundation for dashboards, planning, applications, and AI agents alike? Instead of maintaining one interpretation of the business for dashboards, another for a data platform, and another reconstructed inside every agent, enterprises can work toward shared business meaning, relationships, and governance across these experiences.

For SAP customers, there is another advantage: connecting that context to the business processes where decisions ultimately trigger actions. SAP’s core business data is generated by the processes its customers run, creating an opportunity to combine data and business context as a foundation for enterprise AI.

That is how agentic analytics can shorten the path from data to context, from context to insight, and from insight to decision and action.

Three priorities for data and analytics leaders

  1. Segment analytical workloads. Determine where standardized reporting, exploration, conversation, simulation, and agentic delegation each create the most value.
  2. Design for governed delegation. Define what people consume, what they explore, what they delegate, what requires validation, who approves consequential actions, and how agent behavior, decisions, and outcomes are monitored.
  3. Treat business context as strategic AI infrastructure. Semantic models, metadata, lineage, data products, and knowledge graphs are no longer analytics plumbing. They are the shared infrastructure through which people and AI understand the enterprise.

The next competitive advantage is business context

Dashboards aren’t dead. But they are no longer the boundary of enterprise analytics. Analytics is evolving from something we primarily consume into a capability that can increasingly participate in how work gets done.

Sometimes it will present a dashboard. Sometimes it will answer a question, investigate an anomaly or simulate alternatives. Increasingly, an AI agent will use analytical capabilities to pursue an objective on our behalf.

Access to powerful AI models is not the differentiator; they are improving and commoditizing rapidly. What is far harder to replicate is a trusted understanding of how your business works. That means connecting data with meaning, meaning with relationships, relationships with processes, and AI with the governance required to act responsibly.

This is the future SAP is building toward with SAP Business Data Cloud, SAP Knowledge Graph, SAP Analytics Cloud, and Joule Agents: business context that can ground people and AI as analytics moves closer to the processes that run the enterprise.

Agentic analytics is not the end of dashboards. It is the beginning of something more consequential: turning governed data and insights into trusted decisions and actions.
