How two utilities transformed their operations with SAP Integration Suite

The energy and water sectors are under pressure from every direction (e.g. EU Fit for 55 carbon reporting, CBAM compliance, ENTSO-E market communication mandates, Digital Product Passports). Regulatory requirements are growing more complex. Customer expectations have risen sharply. Smart infrastructure is generating data volumes that legacy systems were never designed to handle. And the integration layer—the connective tissue between billing, metering, asset management, and customer systems—is where transformation succeeds or stalls.

Two utilities have navigated this challenge and come out ahead. Their stories are worth examining in detail.

Severn Trent Water: Replacing Legacy Middleware Before It Became a Crisis

Severn Trent Water is one of the UK's largest water and waste utilities, serving over 4.6 million households across the Midlands and mid-Wales. The company set out to modernize its customer management platform—migrating to Kraken, a cloud-based CRM used by leading energy and water retailers across the UK.

The destination was well chosen. The journey, however, revealed a structural problem.

Severn Trent's existing middleware had been built up incrementally over fifteen years. A web of point-to-point connections linked its CRM, billing system, metering platform, asset management application, and financial systems. Each of those connections represented a dependency, a maintenance burden, and a failure point. When the team began testing the migration, the problems became visible quickly: the middleware could not reliably synchronize approximately 220,000 customer records across Kraken, SAP ECC, Temetra metering, ArcGIS, and the other core systems involved.

The project was at risk—not because the target platform was wrong, but because the integration layer holding everything together could not perform at the scale and reliability the migration required.

Working with Tata Consultancy Services, Severn Trent made the decision to rebuild its integration architecture rather than work around it. The new platform was built on SAP Integration Suite, combining three capabilities:

  • Cloud Integration for modular, reusable integration flows with robust error handling and retry logic
  • API Management for secure, governed exposure of APIs to third-party systems and partners
  • Advanced Event Mesh for high-volume, event-driven processing of customer and metering events

The result was an architecture that replaced fragile point-to-point connections with standardized, governed, reusable flows—one that could grow with the business rather than resist it.

The outcomes were measurable and significant. Time to market for system changes improved by 40%. Customer data alignment across all 220,000 records reached 90%. Billing and asset updates that had previously taken days now happen the same day. Hundreds of IT hours previously consumed by manual reconciliation were recovered and redeployed. The platform runs 24x7 on a smaller infrastructure and energy footprint than the legacy it replaced.

Severn Trent received the SAP Innovation Awards 2026 Technology Pathfinder award for this work—recognition of the architectural quality of the platform and the business outcomes it delivered. ¹

The TCS Integration team have successfully implemented a new strategic integration platform within Severn Trent, using SAP Integration Suite to control message flow between Kraken and SAP to ensure the alignment of customer and financial data across the systems.

— Greg Hammond, Portfolio Manager, Severn Trent

Farys: Making smart meter data actually work

Farys is a Belgian water utility serving the Ghent region, with €454 million in annual revenue. The company made a substantial investment in smart metering infrastructure—deploying sensors across its service territory to provide real-time visibility into water consumption, pressure events, and infrastructure anomalies.

Smart meters only create value when the data they generate can flow to the right systems at the right time. At Farys, that was the gap. Meter readings and events were being collected, but without an integration layer capable of routing them in real time to billing, analytics, customer-facing applications, and predictive maintenance systems, the data was arriving faster than the organization could act on it. Billing still ran on monthly cycles. Leak detection was reactive. The investment in smart infrastructure was not yet translating into the operational and customer experience improvements it was designed to deliver.

Farys addressed this by building a smart water platform on SAP Business AI Platform, with SAP Integration Suite's Advanced Event Mesh at its core. The architecture processes smart meter readings and events as they arrive—routing them immediately to the downstream applications that need them, without the delays and data silos that characterized the previous approach. SAP Cloud for Energy handles data collection, validation, and analysis.

The business impact was immediate and material. Invoice generation lead time dropped from weeks to a single day. Customers gained near-real-time visibility into their own consumption—for many, the first time they had ever been able to see their water usage in anything close to real time. The platform also laid the foundation for AI-driven consumption forecasting and predictive maintenance, capabilities that depend on having clean, timely, and integrated data to work from.

Beyond the operational improvements, the unified platform delivered economic value: Farys achieved a 30% cost reduction through the economies of scale the integrated architecture enabled. ²

With SAP Business Technology Platform (now as BAIP), everything is integrated. Our data is in one place. It unlocks intelligence, simplifies innovation, and lowers our total cost of ownership compared to our peers.

—Inge Opreel, CIO, Farys

What these stories have in common

The challenges Severn Trent and Farys faced were different in their specifics—a migration at risk versus a smart infrastructure investment not yet delivering value—but the root cause was the same: integration gaps that prevented data from reaching the applications that needed it, at the speed and reliability the business required.

Both organizations resolved the problem by building on a capable, scalable integration platform rather than patching the one they had. Both are now running SAP Integration Suite as a strategic layer in their technology stack, not a tactical fix.

For utilities navigating the pressures of the energy and water transition—increasing regulatory complexity, rising customer expectations, new digital infrastructure requirements—the integration layer is where transformation is won or lost. The Severn Trent and Farys outcomes show what is possible when that layer is built right.
