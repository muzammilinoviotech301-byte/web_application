In a recent episode of The Future of ERP podcast, we sat down with Ramesh Chougule, Vice President, SAP Practice Sales and Go to Market North America at Infosys, to talk about a question that a lot of organizations today are asking: Where exactly do we start with AI and cloud ERP, and how do we make sure we're building something that lasts?

Ramesh has spent 25 years working with SAP clients across hundreds of engagements, and he brought a clarity of thinking to this conversation that I think a lot of organizations need right now.

The hype around AI is loud. The question of what to actually do first is harder to answer. This blog pulls out the key themes from our conversation—including Ramesh's practical three-horizon roadmap—and his direct perspective on why the operational core of cloud ERP is more important in the age of AI, not less.

Is AI going to replace ERP?

We opened with the question that Ramesh told us has been driving client conversations for the past two years. His answer was unambiguous:

After a lot of effort, client conversations, analyst conversations, we came to the conclusion that [AI] is not going to replace [ERP] for sure. It's actually going to enhance the ERP. It's going to enhance the backbone of an organization. It is going to continue as a system of records—very important, because that is where the data gets created. So AI is hugely dependent on this particular backbone.

Ramesh Chougule, Infosys

That dependency is the thread that runs through everything Ramesh shared. AI agents need governed, structured, reliable data to act on. Cloud ERP is where that data lives. Organizations that treat them as competing priorities will struggle; organizations that treat cloud ERP as the foundation for AI will compound their advantage.

What is the operational core—and why does it matter for AI?

Ramesh introduced a framework that Infosys has developed based on its extensive work with clients: a three-layer architecture for tomorrow's enterprise. Understanding it helps explain why foundation-setting matters so much right now.

Layer

What it is

AI's role

Operational core

The cloud ERP backbone—finance, supply chain, procurement, HR. The system that keeps the organization running in a disciplined, integrated way, every day.

AI embeds intelligence directly into process execution, at the moment decisions are made.

Strategic core

Innovation-centric functions: R&D, customer insights, marketing strategy, long-term planning. Where organizations define their future products and business models.

AI provides 360-degree insight from internal and external data to accelerate innovation and strategic decision-making.

AI governance layer

The foundational layer that governs both of the above—setting rules for AI adoption, use case prioritization, model selection, and council structures.

Determines how AI is deployed safely, consistently, and with measurable outcomes.

On the relationship between the operational and strategic layers, Ramesh was direct:

The strategy cannot be developed without having very good operational data, which is generated from the business processes on a day-to-day basis. All that data is going to provide the right business context... All that data integrity from the processes which are generated by ERP is going to define how good the strategic core is going to function.

Ramesh Chougule, Infosys

In other words, the quality of an organization's AI-powered strategy is only as good as the data discipline in its operational core. Clean ERP data isn't a precondition for AI—it's a competitive advantage.

Context-native AI built into your application suite outperforms external AI platforms bolted on top. What that means for your AI strategy.

What does AI in cloud ERP look like in practice?

Ramesh gave concrete examples of where AI is already changing day-to-day operations—not as a future possibility, but as something his teams are seeing with clients now:

  • A procurement manager receiving the best available contract price at the moment they're creating a purchase requisition—not after a manual lookup, but in real time, embedded in the transaction.
  • A collections agent triggering an escalation automatically, without human intervention, to ensure receivables are received on time.
  • Autonomous warehousing, where multi-agent AI orchestrates picking, packing, and shipping on the warehouse floor—avoiding stock-out situations and maximizing order fulfilment in real time.

On that last example, Ramesh pointed to Infosys's demonstration at SAP Sapphire 2026:

At Sapphire 2026, Infosys had an AI booth... where we had displayed autonomous warehouse, where multi-agents were orchestrating how the intelligence is coming into the warehouse floor, helping the warehouse operators to intelligently pick, pack, and ship the goods in a real-time manner, avoiding stock-out situations, ensuring order fulfillment to the highest of its possibility.

Ramesh Chougule, Infosys

What connects these examples is the principal Ramesh kept returning to: AI delivers value when it's embedded in the process at the moment of execution—not sitting on top of the data, but acting within it.

What is the short- and long-term roadmap for AI and cloud ERP?

This is the practical framework I'd encourage any business or IT leader to take away from this conversation. Ramesh described how Infosys structures AI adoption roadmaps for clients across three time horizons:

Horizon

Timeline

What to focus on

Now

0–12 months

Prepare your data. Build data lakes. Identify low-hanging use cases. Embed intelligence into high-frequency, rules-based processes. Establish foundational AI governance: define which models you'll use, how decisions get made, and who owns them.

Near

1–2 years

Accelerate agent development. Scale from ~20 agents to a broader portfolio. Rebalance the work breakdown structure between humans and agents based on what you've learned. HR, finance, and procurement are the natural early anchors.

Next

2+ years

Exponential scale. Every process becomes a candidate for AI adoption. Governance is mature enough to handle that volume. The strategic core is established and on a maturation journey. The question shifts from 'which use case?' to 'how do we scale?'

On why the 'now' phase tends to be underestimated:

In the last one year, I have had at least 200 conversations on SAP BDC, and for every conversation, the fulcrum has been to prepare data for your AI to generate the outcomes which you envisage from that investment.

Ramesh Chougule, Infosys

It's a point that's easy to gloss over when the excitement is about agents and automation. But Ramesh is clear: organizations that skip the data and governance groundwork in the "now" phase will find that their AI investments underdeliver—regardless of how sophisticated the technology is.

How should organizations think about build versus buy?

One of the most practical questions we asked was about the build versus buy decision—particularly relevant for organizations being pitched custom AI solutions when embedded options already exist in their ERP platform.

Ramesh shared the governing principle Infosys uses with clients:

Our first point to go is the AI catalog in the SAP Discovery Center. Identify which AI solutions are already in place as part of our process analysis. We analyzed all 300-plus AI solutions which are part of today's embedded AI suite from SAP, and advise the clients that this is your first option, the first preference. Let's identify how suitable it is for your operations and requirements today, and make an effort to adopt that first.

Ramesh Chougule, Infosys

Custom AI development is the second option—reserved for requirements that are genuinely specific to an organization's competitive differentiation and not covered by embedded solutions. And even then, Ramesh is disciplined about it:

  • Value analysis upfront—what outcome does this custom build produce, and is it measurable?
  • Architectural analysis upfront—how does this fit within the existing ERP and governance landscape?
  • Five-year cost projection—including the often-overlooked cost of running custom AI (inference costs, model maintenance, token costs).

This is a framework that many organizations don't apply rigorously enough, particularly in the early enthusiasm of AI experimentation. The result, as Effie noted during the conversation, is pilot purgatory—AI investments that generate learning but not value. Ramesh's advice is to be intentional from the start about what success looks like and how you'll measure it.

What is the future of ERP?

We always end the podcast with the same question—and Ramesh's answer was worth quoting in full:

ERP is here to stay—and not only here to stay, but to enhance, become a lot more intelligent, bringing in the operational intelligence, sentience, and responsiveness to the next level for organizations to function even more effectively and in much better serving their end customers. It's going to be a critical foundation for the strategic core to get established. It's going to be the backbone. I must say, it's going to be an intelligent backbone going forward.

Ramesh Chougule, Infosys

That phrase—intelligent backbone—captures something important. The organizations that will get the most from AI are not the ones that replace their operational foundations, but the ones that strengthen them and build on top. The data, process integrity, and governance that cloud ERP provides aren't constraints on AI ambition. They're what make AI ambition achievable.

Three things to take away from this conversation

  1. Getting your data right is not optional. It's the single most common bottleneck Ramesh sees in AI engagements—and it's the work that has to happen in the 'now' phase, before agents can deliver meaningful value.
  2. Start with embedded AI before building custom. SAP's portfolio of 300+ embedded AI solutions is the most practical starting point for most organizations. Custom development should be reserved for genuine competitive differentiation—and evaluated rigorously on cost versus value.
  3. Governance isn't a back-office task. The AI governance layer Ramesh describes is what allows organizations to scale from 20 agents to enterprise-wide adoption without losing control of quality, compliance, or cost.

FAQ

  • What is AI cloud ERP?

    AI cloud ERP refers to cloud-based ERP platforms that are enhanced with artificial intelligence, including platforms with AI embedded directly into business processes, where AI agents automate decisions and actions in finance, supply chain, procurement, and HR.

    SAP Cloud ERP is a leading example. SAP Cloud ERP supplies the stable transaction backbone and business context, while SAP Joule and the SAP Business AI Platform enable intelligence and insights that enhance productivity and responsiveness across the business. SAP Cloud ERP also has more than 300 embedded AI solutions.

  • How should companies start their AI and ERP journey?

    According to Infosys, the most important first step is data readiness—building clean, governed data foundations and identifying high-frequency, rules-based processes where AI can deliver immediate value. Establishing an AI governance structure early is equally critical.

  • Should companies build or buy AI for ERP?

    Infosys recommends starting with embedded AI solutions from the SAP Discovery Portal before considering custom development. Custom AI should be reserved for requirements that represent genuine competitive differentiation and evaluated on a rigorous cost-versus-value basis, including a five-year projection of build and run costs.

  • Will AI replace ERP?

    No. Both SAP and Infosys agree that AI depends on the structured data, process logic, and governance that ERP provides. Without that foundation, AI agents cannot deliver reliable business outcomes.
