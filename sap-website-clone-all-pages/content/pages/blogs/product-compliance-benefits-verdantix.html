How product compliance software benefits business outcomes

Perspectives from Verdantix Green Quadrant for Product Compliance Software (2025)

Shifting compliance from cost center to business advantage

For manufacturers and distributors, product compliance is no longer just a box to check—it’s a critical business function. Especially for sustainable supply chain processes, the regulatory landscape is evolving rapidly, with new requirements emerging across global markets nearly every day. From chemical safety and dangerous goods to digital product passports and ESG mandates, the stakes have never been higher in regulatory compliance for products. When companies fall out of compliance, results can include:

  • Delayed or blocked shipments
  • Costly recalls and fines
  • Exclusion from key markets
  • Damaged brand reputation

But what if compliance could be more than a defensive play and enable tangible business benefits as well? The new Verdantix Green Quadrant: Product Compliance Software 2025 report shows how leading organizations are transforming compliance from a cost center to a source of operational efficiency, risk mitigation, and market advantage. In addition, the report identifies how leading solution providers compare across a range of industries and scenarios.

Sustainable supply chains require sustainable processes

According to Verdantix, the number of firms prioritizing proactive management of hazardous substances has jumped significantly in just the past year. They show interest in enhancing processes to meet regulations surrounding persistent organic pollutants (POPs) and per-and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS), in addition to identifying less dangerous chemical alternatives. Moreover, new compliance requirements for sustainability such as the EU’s CSRD, EUDR, and CBAM, along with California’s Proposition 65 initiative are introducing an extra layer of compliance complexity.

Companies that historically relied on manual compliance checks are quickly discovering their teams and processes are stretched thin. They're under pressure to keep up—not just with regulations, but with the data, documentation, and supplier engagement that these frameworks often demand. Here are two common examples:

Internal scenario: A company modifies the chemical formulation of a product. Any time a formulation changes or a new regulation is added in a jurisdiction where it's sold, product marketability assessments and all corresponding safety data sheets must be updated in a timely manner. Failure to do so can lead to losing market access, safety incidents, or fines.

External scenario: A company's supplier switches to using a noncompliant component. The supplier could fail to notify its manufacturing customer, or the internal compliance team may not immediately notice the change and fail to take appropriate, timely action.

Incidents like these can leave companies vulnerable, including those running modern, cloud-based ERP solutions. Despite using the latest features and automating a portion of production steps, many still struggle with fragmented processes, manual checks, and data silos between R&D, procurement, product compliance, and operations. This can slow product launches, increase risk, and even threaten revenue.
