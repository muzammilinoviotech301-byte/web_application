Where is my “Flywheel”?

By the way, for anyone who wondered where the “Flywheel” graphic went: it was the central element in the visualization of SAP's Business Suite strategy in 2025. The logic behind it remains fully relevant in the new context.

The “Flywheel” and it’s new visual

The suite applications run the business processes and, in doing so, generate application data. This data needs to be gathered, optimized, harmonized, and stored in a good way in BDC to support a cross-system execution of Joule Assistants and Joule Agents. Which means to enable the driving elements of the Autonomous Enterprise. The outcomes of these agentic processes trigger business suite transactions, closing the circle once again. In presentations of the Autonomous Enterprise, the flywheel theme is reflected across every domain of SAP Assistants—both those which are already available and those still to be delivered.

According to Gartner (Source: Gartner (2025).“Predict 2026: Intelligent Applications”), “by 2030, no more than 35% of organizations will have sufficient data quality to take advantage of advanced AI in their applications”. Actually, most AI initiatives underdeliver not because the technology fails, but because organisations underestimate the change investment required to realizbue value. SAP's architecture addresses this directly. Business semantics, governance, and process context are already embedded in the platform—rather than having to be reconstructed from raw extracts. This way, organisations can direct their change investment where it creates most impact. It could be building agent skill, redesigning processes around new decision flows, and embedding change sustainably across lines of business. The result is a faster path to measurable value, and a materially lower risk of the adoption failures that account for most of AI initiative underperformance.

Let’s look at the data side of this process. For many organizations and enterprises, the question arises whether it might not be possible to build Industry Intelligence simply by extracting raw data and placing an agentic layer on top. And this is what many SAP customers do today. This is of course possible, but not the preferable approach. To preserve the business semantics within the AI models, business context and governance must remain intact. Using the SAP Business AI Platform as the architecture allows you to do exactly that. The next section will explain this for you. But beforehand, let’s clarify something.

Why Agents, Why SAP Agents and Assistants?

To start with a fundamental question: why is there a need for agents at all? In daily life, Large Language Models (LLMs) like ChatGPT or Gemini can solve nearly anything. Limitations are not so easy to see but if you calculate two large numbers (five or six digits long), which are usual in business, you will find plausible but mathematically wrong answers (try it yourself!). You find out: LLMs cannot calculate. Only combined with a calculation tool, you get the right answers.

Enterprise decisions are rarely simple lookups—they are multi-step, cross-system, and consequential. A procurement decision depends on supplier performance, inventory levels, contract terms, and budget position simultaneously. A financial accrual requires understanding process context, business rules, and authorization boundaries across lines of business. General-purpose AI models, however capable, cannot reliably navigate this complexity: they lack access to a live transactional context, cannot enforce business-specific rules and authorizations, and have no mechanism to trigger actions in the underlying systems.

This fact is exactly why agents exist. An agent combines an AI model's reasoning capability with the ability to call the right tools at the right time. It queries live data, applies business logic, respects governance boundaries, and executes consequential actions within the system itself.

Accuracy at each step matters—and compounds. Industry standards cite 80–90% accuracy for individual agents; across three sequential agentic steps, compounded reliability drops to 51–73%. For business-critical decisions executed at scale—vendor selection, financial accruals, commercialization strategy—this is not a rounding error. It is the difference between automation that creates value and automation that creates liability. This is why SAP agents are designed around the complete data ontology, semantic accuracy, and governance from the ground up, not added as a layer on top.

Why use SAP Agents or agents built within the SAP-defined architecture?

The common approach of many customers is straightforward. They own a customized ERP system surrounded by other siloed applications, have some AI experiences, and ask—would it not make sense to simply add an AI architecture on top, plug in agents, extract data from the system, and return a recommendation or execute it via an API?

This approach, however, comes with at least three disadvantages:

First, it involves a loss of semantics. When raw tables are extracted, relationships, hierarchies, currencies, and business logic are lost—the data becomes flat and silent. Semantics must be brought back to life before the data can be used properly.

Second, this setup comes with a high rebuilding tax. Significant manual effort is required to reconstruct business logic, joins, and security context—all of which are lost during extraction. Only with knowledge of the ontology is it possible to interpret the information correctly, evaluate business process steps and outcomes, and produce reliable recommendations. Besides this, additional lifecycle costs and costs for tokens to use the AI components need to be added to the calculation.

Third, remote agents operate in contextual blindness. There is no comprehensive view or unified access across different applications. Agents cannot anticipate implications from or with other applications. When data is extracted from a single application without cross-contextual information—such as APIs and process flows—that context is gone.

For a customer who wants to use agents, it is essential           these understand the data in the system. It is important to understand, for example, that customers are related to their own attributes (customer number, address, contacts, and so on). And equally important to understand what these customers can trigger (requesting a service or product, transferring money to an account). Combined with the data behind these relations, the accuracy of any recommendation of an agent can be maximized. And as mentioned already, agentic processes often cascade over multiple recommendations, any loss of accuracy is multiplied.

Of course, there is a still positive side to this plug-in setup: since it is separated from the application, it is straightforward to implement agents in a flexible environment, independent of the complexity of existing system boundaries. Sometimes the complexity of such decoupled processes is low and therefore the accuracy is less important—but in any case, this work-around comes with risk.

So, how does the SAP Business AI Platform architecture differentiate itself from that of many other AI companies in the SAP Autonomous Suite context? What exactly enables proper agent usage? And what is the role of SAP BDC here?

The following three pillars highlight unique capabilities. To replicate these in a third-party environment for Assistants and Agents, continuous effort is required—or they simply cannot be replaced:

  1. It is possible to capture the full context of the suite and beyond. The customer's specific data is maintained in a comprehensive semantic structure. Harmonization is built on a Data Product-based data model in SAP BDC. SAP's assistants and agents can understand how data is related across Lines of Business (LoBs) using the SAP-managed knowledge graph (capturing the entire ontology) over this layer in an entity-relation model.
  2. The entire architecture is interwoven with an integrated Governance Framework. It is supporting legal requirements (including full audit trail, GDPR capabilities, fulfilling the EU AI Act pathway, and so forth), implemented industry standards and best practices, as well as the roles, responsibilities, and authorizations across the entire connected suite. All of this is deeply embedded, interconnected and used in every application and in the Business AI Platform itself.
  3. The outcome of any agentic processing is usually a recommendation, leading to an execution task in the underlying system. As this process is implemented in a fully integrated way—inside the system context and without relying on an external API—this step is uniquely implemented within the SAP architecture. Beyond this, there is always a human in the loop to ensure control of the process, drawing on business and process experience and enabling a comprehensive assessment of any business situation.

The three pillars: how SAP's Business AI Platform architecture differs from others

For pillars 1) and 2), BDC is essential to delivering SAP's vision of the Autonomous Enterprise across all agents, applications, data sources, and the full technical platform. Pillar 3) can be seen as less immediately critical; however, the most impactful recommendations are grounded in analytical data—and BDC is the optimal source for this. To address all three differentiators, the central component is the "Knowledge Core" as part of the SAP Business AI Platform. It defines what data means to the business and ensures that context is applied consistently across the entire landscape.

SAP's integrated agentic approach (architecture overview)

With this architecture, agent execution is grounded in Context, Semantics, and Governance. This delivers various advantages:

  1. To answer business questions or execute business tasks across SAP and non-SAP data sources with ease, all necessary information is contained in harmonized data models in SAP Business Data Cloud using SAP-managed Data Products. The master data is aligned using SAP Reltio capabilities. The alignment of the different suite data models is made possible using SAP Dremio.
  2. To retrieve data and execute these processes with high performance and accuracy, the knowledge graph—built on top of these data models—makes it possible to find the right information. Here, the required relationships, which can be derived from Joule prompts or formulated by the agents, resonate with the underlying relationship model.
  3. To execute all of this within one integrated Governance Framework—fully respecting system governance such as authorizations, industry standards, auditability, and regulations—enables professional, reliable, and secure processing, where all functions are grounded in the same role-and-responsibility concept and a defined, legally compliant governance framework.

Summary

Using the described architecture and the content built by SAP, it is possible to fuel and execute on SAP's strategy and vision for the Autonomous Suite. Without the Business AI Platform, and in particular without BDC, this would not be possible. It is a differentiating architecture that helps any organization follow the path toward the Autonomous Enterprise—executing quickly, accurately, and securely. The integration of data, ontology, and governance across the Autonomous Suite and the Business AI Platform is unique and very powerful.

The potential of using agents in business processes is very visible in general. You can accelerate a decision process when you interpret large amounts of data in an instant to find out more quickly which vendor to choose or which commercialization strategy to apply. But without accuracy of the agents’ recommendations, these values can quickly turn into threats.
