From digital hype to pragmatic transformation

A new study by SAP’s Industry Transformation Advisory and Kühne Logistics University shows how supply chain management will evolve by 2035: more connected, data-driven, and adaptable. Drawing on the latest research, the experience from SAP’s Business Consultants, and surveys of 660 supply chain experts, the research tracks a clear shift from past tech-driven euphoria to pragmatic, use-case-led transformation – where value must be proven before scaling.

The starting point is a study that SAP conducted ten years ago with researchers from RWTH Aachen. Back then, the digital hype promised sweeping change – 3D printing, augmented reality (AR), IoT, social data forecasts, and drone delivery. Reality proved slower realization, caused by underestimated complexity, immature solutions, skills gaps, and inconsistent data often stalled progress, among other issues. Pioneers still succeeded, typically by targeting specific, high-ROI use cases that improved delivery reliability, reduced disruptions, and created tangible user value.

However, external shocks like the pandemic, geopolitics, or economic volatility have reinforced a new discipline. As indicated within a recent follow-up study, companies invest only when benefits are clear, start small, and scale after results are proven. The winners are building the foundations for a truly digital supply chain: high-quality data, integrated platforms, and teams fluent in AI.

Looking into the future, the present study paints a vision for the supply chains of the future and identifies "no-regret moves" companies should act on now. The logic is simple and follows the recommendation of Wayne Gretzky, who said that good players skate to the puck, while great players to where the puck is going to be. Applied to supply chains, this means strengthening data governance, standardizing processes, and systematically introducing employees to AI tools before the pressure to do so becomes unavoidable.

The study consists of deep dives into 5 key areas: Plan, Source, Make, Deliver, and People. Here are key highlights for each area.

Plan: AI-driven supply chain planning

By 2035, planning will look fundamentally different – more intelligent, more integrated, and more automated.

  • Planning is becoming a data-driven early warning and control system: Integrated, multi-horizon planning anchored in high-quality, harmonized data and a company-wide IBP (Integrated Business Planning) and S&OP (Sales and Operations Planning) backbone is expected to be decisive by 2035; lacking this integration correlates with loss of operational control and competitiveness.
  • AI and autonomous agents will materially reshape planning: Forecasting accuracy and proactive risk detection rise with expanded data sources; planning becomes simpler and more intuitive as agents automate repetitive tasks and shift human roles toward analysis and decisions – yet most firms today remain far from this state due to data and maturity gaps.
  • Operating models will diverge while end-to-end integration advances: Strategic, bespoke planning gravitates to agent-based AI, whereas standardized environments trend toward market-standard platforms and planning-as-a-service; cross-company planning, simulations, and AI-assisted digital twins become practical as modeling agents reduce complexity and improve scalability.

Source: Data, autonomous sourcing, and the strategic supply chain

By 2035, today’s routine purchasing tasks will run fully autonomously, freeing professionals for strategic and risk mitigating work.

  • Procurement is changing under stronger macro pressures and widespread AI. Geopolitics, sustainability, raw-material scarcity, and the battle for top talents matter more. Integrated apps with semantic data, AI agents, analytics, and business network signals start to shape every process, with impact differing by industry and business model.
  • Vision exceeds today’s maturity. By 2035, many expect a bigger strategic role for procurement and better AI-driven outcomes with high-quality data. Yet today, only 12% report a C-level integration and 3% strong AI analytics. On the other hand, 28% expect a strategic role for procurement and 31% better AI outcomes, revealing a large execution gap.
  • End-to-end data and process design are the decisive enabler for AI in sourcing. Category strategies managed in apps, standardized BPMN processes, modern integrated purchasing tools, and a shared semantic data model enable full visibility and analytics. As data quality improves, adoption of AI agents and orchestration across buyers, requestors, and suppliers significantly increases, driving more agent-to-agent interactions across networks.

Make: AI, digital twins, and the future of smart manufacturing

By 2035, many organizations need to overcome the gap between their bright ambitions for 2035 and the maturity of their current digital environment.

  • Production is the nerve center of the future supply chain, driven by data, networks, and the pursuit of flexibility. Real-time data and AI link design, manufacturing, logistics, and recycling. While geopolitics, energy volatility, and sustainability pressures push regionalization, cost discipline remains essential.
  • Ambition exceeds current maturity. Leaders describe a connected, AI-enabled, and sustainable factory for 2035, yet execution today is fragmented. The most significant gaps are digital continuity and interoperability, clean and connected data, circular models and closed-loop flows, and the step from AI pilots to scale.
  • The 2035 factory is autonomous, modular, and circular. AI orchestrates material flows and schedules; mobile robots and self-learning systems adapt in real time; digital twins and IoT create a lifecycle memory for each product – enabling traceability, predictive maintenance, precise material returns, and continuous design improvement. Human roles shift to strategic, creative, and supervisory work, and scaling requires new skills, structures, and leadership, with progress coming from integrated data, technologies, and processes rather than isolated experiments.

Deliver: Real-Time Visibility, AI Agents, and Supply Chain Resilience

By 2035, global competition will drive logistics to become more efficient, transparent, and cost-effective.

  • Logistics sits at the heart of cost, service, and resilience. Tight digital integration across transport and warehousing prevents bottlenecks, reduces costs, and boosts service. AI, robotics, and digital platforms are ready and proving value: tightly integrating transport, yard, and warehouse unlocks speed, reliability, and transparency – preventing bottlenecks, reducing costs, and elevating service even as energy, transport, and labor pressures rise.
  • By 2035, 39% of our surveyed experts expect data-driven warehouses, 28% expect autonomous vehicles or drones, 28% see AI agents supporting dispatchers and warehouse teams, and 20% expect real-time cross-company ecosystems. However, today, only a small proportion use real-time data comprehensively in warehousing, achieve real-time transport transparency with accurate ETAs, or have collaborative planning with partners.
  • The greatest gains will accrue in environments with end-to-end, real-time data and robust orchestration; in these settings, AI agents and flexible robotics deliver measurable improvements in estimated time of arrival (ETA) accuracy, utilization, and error rates, while firms with fragmented systems and a cost-center mindset progress slowly despite mature technology.

People: New skills, new roles for Supply Chain Talent in 2035

Supply chain managers are moving away from reactive daily tasks – like reviewing schedules and fixing delivery issues – toward a data-driven, predictive role that anticipates problems before they arise.

  • Cloud platforms and AI copilots amplify human impact. Automation clears routine work so teams can focus on strategic, end-to-end orchestration; tighter human-tech collaboration lifts speed, resilience, and transparency despite talent constraints.
  • Decisions will move from execution to design, reaction to anticipation, and KPIs to outcomes. The control tower's morning review surfaces risks and opportunities at a glance, AI agents propose, AI agents propose scenarios, people adjust policies, run Digital Twin experiments, and make a few high-impact calls – supported by new roles like Network Orchestrator, Scenario Strategist, and Data Steward.
  • The importance of developing T-shaped skills (a combination of broad, cross-functional knowledge and deep expertise in one specific area) will become even more significant. Combining broad end-to-end process understanding, data and digital fluency, and cross-functional collaboration with deep expertise in priority domains such as planning, logistics, sourcing, or customer service. With sharpened strategic judgment, a strong sense of responsibility, and the ability to orchestrate ecosystems, the supply chain teams of the future will turn tools into outcomes.

Conclusion: Transformation starts now

The research is clear: to lead in 2035, start now. Competitive advantage in 2035 will not be won by technology alone. It will be built on fundamentals: data quality, process discipline, and human capability. Companies that invest in these foundations now create the conditions for AI and automation to deliver real value, not just proof-of-concepts. The supply chain of 2035 will belong to those who treated today as a starting line.

FAQ

  • What is the SAP-KLU Supply Chain 2035 study?

    It is a joint research project by SAP Industry Transformation Advisory (ITA) and Kühne Logistics University that defines a vision for the supply chains of 2035 across planning, procurement, manufacturing, logistics, and talent. Driven by the rise of emerging technologies and a strategic shift from cost-only thinking toward resilience and sustainability, it combines consulting expertise, SAP product input, and a practitioner survey to identify concrete actions companies should take to future-proof their supply chains.

  • How was the "Supply Chain of the Future" study conducted?

    The study combined three complementary inputs: the collective experience of SAP's ITA Supply Chain consultants together with academic partners (KLU and KIT), alignment sessions with SAP Product teams to incorporate their product roadmap perspectives, and a practitioner survey of more than 660 experts designed to capture the gap between companies' current reality and future potential. This triangulation of consulting practice, academic research, and direct practitioner input ensures the findings reflect both strategic vision and operational ground truth.

  • What are the biggest supply chain trends expected by 2035?

    The study points to five defining shifts: AI-driven planning, autonomous procurement shaped by geopolitics and sustainability pressures, modular manufacturing, real-time logistics orchestration, and a workforce built around T-shaped skills and human-AI collaboration. Across all five, high-quality data and integrated platforms are the decisive enablers.

  • How will AI change supply chain planning?

    By 2035, AI and autonomous agents are expected to handle repetitive planning tasks: improving forecast accuracy, detecting risks earlier, and automating routine decisions. Human roles shift toward analysis, policy design, and high-impact calls. Most companies are still far from this state today, primarily due to data quality and integration gaps.

  • What does the factory of 2035 look like?

    The study describes it as autonomous, modular, and circular. AI orchestrates material flows and production schedules; mobile robots and self-learning systems adapt in real time; digital twins and IoT maintain a lifecycle memory for every product. Human roles concentrate on strategic, creative, and supervisory work rather than routine execution.

  • How is logistics expected to change by 2035?

    Survey respondents expect significant advances: 39% anticipate data-driven warehouses, 28% foresee autonomous vehicles or drones, and 28% expect AI agents supporting dispatchers and warehouse teams. However, today only a few achieve real-time transport transparency with accurate ETAs–indicating a substantial execution gap still to close.

  • What skills will supply chain professionals need in 2035?

    The study emphasizes T-shaped skills: broad end-to-end process understanding and data fluency combined with deep expertise in a specific domain such as planning, logistics, or procurement. New roles like Network Orchestrator, Scenario Strategist, and Data Steward are expected to emerge as teams shift from execution to strategic orchestration.

  • What should companies do right now to prepare for 2035?

    Start with the fundamentals: invest in data quality and governance, standardize core processes, and build AI fluency across your teams. The study's central finding is that companies waiting for perfect conditions or proven large-scale results will fall behind those already building the foundations needed to scale when the technology matures—data, platforms, and people.
