Frequently asked questions

Workforce planning is a strategic process focused on forecasting future talent needs and aligning them with business objectives to ensure the right skills are available at the right time. It emphasizes agility, long-term planning, and the use of analytics and AI to anticipate workforce trends.

 

Workforce management, on the other hand, deals with day-to-day operational tasks such as scheduling, time tracking, and managing current staff. While workforce management ensures efficiency in the present, workforce planning prepares organizations for future challenges and opportunities. 

The workforce planning process typically follows five core steps:

  • Assess demand: Forecast future workforce needs based on business goals and growth plans.

  • Analyze supply: Review current workforce capabilities, demographics, and skills.

  • Identify gaps: Compare projected demand and supply to uncover shortages or surpluses.

  • Develop strategies: Define interventions such as hiring, reskilling, or automation to close gaps.

  • Monitor and adjust: Continuously track progress and refine plans as conditions change.

Common models include:

  • Skills-based models, which focus on capabilities rather than job titles, enable organizations to plan for evolving skill requirements.

  • Scenario-based models, which evaluate multiple future scenarios to understand potential risks, costs, and workforce implications.

  • AI-driven models, which use predictive analytics to dynamically forecast workforce demand and supply, improving accuracy and agility.

Workforce planning tools integrate HR, finance, and business data to create a unified view of workforce needs. Solutions like workforce planning in SAP Enterprise Planning enable real-time collaboration, predictive forecasting, and scenario modeling. This integration helps organizations respond quickly to market changes, optimize costs, and ensure talent readiness to achieve strategic goals.

Buy/bot/borrow refers to three approaches organizations use to close skills gaps:

  • Buy: Hire new talent externally to bring in critical skills.

  • Bot: Automate tasks using AI or robotics to reduce manual work and increase efficiency.

  • Borrow: Use contingent workers or flexible talent models to meet short-term or specialized needs. 

This strategy helps businesses remain agile and cost-effective while adapting to rapid shifts in skill requirements.

AI is transforming workforce planning through agentic capabilities across the entire planning lifecycle. Planning agents create plans and scenarios grounded in business objectives. Monitoring agents continuously track headcount gaps, cost variances, and skill shortages. Recommendation agents provide actionable guidance—proposing whether to hire, reskill, redeploy, or adjust the workforce mix. Together, these agents replace static planning cycles with a continuously informed process that combines AI insight with human judgment.
