Digitization vs. digitalization: Key differences

Before exploring the broader impact of digital transformation, it’s essential to distinguish between two often-confused concepts: digitization and digitalization. The difference is more than semantic—it helps organizations understand whether they’re simply modernizing infrastructure or actively transforming how they work and compete.

What is digitization? Definition and examples

Digitization is the process of converting analog information into digital formats, enabling data to be stored, accessed, and processed by computers and digital systems.

In a business context, digitization often involves transforming physical records like printed invoices, contracts, or handwritten notes into structured digital data that can be integrated into enterprise systems. Scanning paper-based customer files into a digital database or converting analog sensor readings into digital signals for real-time monitoring are common digitization practices.

The origins of digitization trace back to the early days of computing, when businesses began using mainframes to store and process data previously kept in filing cabinets or on paper ledgers. Over time, the practice evolved alongside advances in hardware and software, becoming more sophisticated and widespread.

Today, digitization is a foundational step in digital transformation, enabling organizations to unlock efficiencies, improve data accessibility, and lay the groundwork for automation and analytics. While it doesn’t inherently change how a business operates, it is a critical enabler for more advanced digital initiatives.

What is digitalization? Definition and examples

Digitalization is the use of digital technologies to transform business operations, models, and customer experiences.

Once digitization has converted analog data into digital formats, digitalization leverages that digital data to drive innovation, efficiency, and strategic change. In practice, this might look like automating supply chain workflows using AI, implementing predictive analytics to optimize customer engagement, or redesigning service delivery through digital platforms.

The concept of digitalization gained momentum as businesses began to realize that simply having digital data wasn’t enough—they needed to use it to rethink how they operate. Early examples include enterprise resource planning (ERP) systems that integrated and centralized data across business departments, enabling more agile decision-making.

Today, digitalization is central to digital transformation strategies, encompassing cloud computing, machine learning, Internet of Things (IoT), and more. It’s not just about tools—it’s about reimagining processes to be faster, smarter, and more responsive to change.

Why the distinction between digitization and digitalization matters

Understanding the difference between digitization and digitalization is crucial for organizations aiming to thrive in a digital-first world. While digitization is a critical transformation step, it is largely technical and does not intrinsically alter an organization’s operations.

Digitalization, on the other hand, involves using digital tech to transform business processes, models, and customer experiences. It’s about leveraging digital tools to create new value, improve efficiency, and enable innovation.

The distinction matters because conflating the two can lead to missed opportunities and misaligned strategies. A company that focuses solely on digitization may end up with digital records but still rely on outdated workflows. In contrast, digitalization encourages rethinking how work gets done—automating tasks, enabling remote collaboration, and using data to drive decisions.

Recognizing this difference helps leaders set clearer goals, allocate resources more effectively, and measure progress in terms of transformation rather than just conversion.

Why digitalization is important for businesses

The technologies underlying digitalization continue to evolve rapidly, even as they are being adopted throughout business and society. Digitalization, however, is not simply a matter of “more technology.” Digitalization is important because it unlocks new thinking and approaches in how a business perceives its role within its ecosystem and its opportunity for increased profitability. The technology is not an end in itself.

With digitalization in place, companies can begin to create new value chains and experiences that are collaborative, interactive, sustainable, and profitable.

Here are some examples of enterprises that have used digitalization to deliver tangible business value:

  • Nestlé, one of the world’s largest food and beverage companies, undertook a global IT transformation to standardize ERP systems and migrate to the cloud. The initiative delivered a 99.97% system availability, enabled one unified digital entry point for 275,000 employees, and moved over 1,200 TB of data to cloud infrastructure.
  • The Mercedes-AMG PETRONAS Formula One Team enhanced racing performance and resource efficiency using real‑time analytics and cost-cap management. By building a robust cloud environment, they optimized physical and financial operations while maintaining elite performance standards.
  • The band Coldplay launched an app to help minimize the environmental impact of its global concert tour and track fan travel-related carbon emissions. The app achieved over 1 million downloads, offered real-time sustainability insights, and encouraged greener travel choices across more than 150 countries.

Enabling technologies: Cloud, AI, IoT, and automation

Digitalization is powered by a set of transformative technologies that enable businesses to operate more intelligently, efficiently, and responsively, even while on the go:

  • Cloud computing: Provides scalable infrastructure and real-time access to data and applications, allowing organizations to break down silos and collaborate across geographies
  • AI: Enhances decision-making by analyzing vast datasets, identifying patterns, and automating complex tasks—from customer service chatbots to predictive maintenance
  • IoT: Connects physical assets to digital systems, enabling real-time monitoring, data collection, and remote control of equipment and environments
  • Automation: Streamlines repetitive processes, reduces human error, and accelerates workflows—freeing up teams to focus on strategic initiatives

Together, these technologies form the backbone of digitalization, enabling organizations to innovate, adapt, and scale in a fast-changing digital landscape.

Business benefits of digitalization

Digitalization offers a wide range of strategic and operational benefits that can significantly enhance an organization’s competitiveness and agility. By integrating digital technologies into core business processes, companies can streamline workflows, reduce manual effort, and improve overall efficiency. For example, automating routine tasks such as invoice processing or inventory management not only saves time but also minimizes errors and frees up resources for higher-value work.

Beyond operational gains, digitalization enables better decision-making through real-time data access and advanced analytics. Businesses can respond more quickly to market changes, personalize customer experiences, and uncover new revenue opportunities.

Digitalization also supports scalability, allowing organizations to adapt and grow without being constrained by legacy systems. Ultimately, digitalization is not just about adopting new tools—it’s about creating a more responsive, data-driven, and innovative business environment that creates value through:

  • Improved productivity
  • Reduced risk
  • Increased scalability
  • Higher cross-regional consistency

Digitalization challenges and risks

While digitalization offers significant benefits, it also introduces challenges and risks that organizations must navigate carefully. Common hurdles include:

  • Integration complexity: Ensuring that new digital tools work seamlessly with existing infrastructure can require substantial time, investment, and expertise—especially when legacy systems are involved.
  • Market expectations of digitalized services: Companies face increasing pressure to provide more services in a digital form, such as online or mobile apps. This requires proactive planning to predict needs and pick strategies that future-proof the business for the next digital wave.
  • Technology overreach: Overreach can occur when businesses invest in tools without a clear strategy, often resulting in underutilized systems or fragmented processes.
  • Data security and privacy: As digital systems handle increasing volumes of sensitive information, organizations become more vulnerable to cyber threats and compliance issues—risks that they can’t afford to ignore.
  • Change management: Digitalization often requires shifts in culture, roles, and workflows, which can meet resistance from employees or leadership. Without clear communication and training, adoption may stall.

To mitigate these risks, businesses must approach digitalization with a well-defined roadmap, strong governance, and a focus on aligning technology with business goals.

How to move from digitization to digitalization: A roadmap

Moving from digitization to digitalization is not just a technical upgrade—it’s a strategic transformation. Digitization lays the groundwork, but digitalization builds on that foundation to reimagine how work is done, decisions are made, and value is delivered. For modern enterprises, this shift requires a clear roadmap that integrates technology with business goals.

  1. Cloud ERP
    The first step is investing in a cloud ERP. Whether you're a first-time buyer of an ERP system or want to migrate from an on-premises one, a cloud ERP enables scalable infrastructure, centralized data access, and real-time collaboration across teams and geographies. Cloud ERP platforms eliminate the limitations of on-premises systems and provide the flexibility needed to innovate quickly.
  2. Automation
    Next, organizations should implement automation through software, applications, and integrated systems. This includes automating repetitive tasks, streamlining workflows, and connecting systems to reduce friction and improve efficiency. Automation helps unlock the full potential of digitized data by making processes faster and more intelligent.
  3. Mobile enablement
    Going mobile is essential for enabling a modern, flexible workforce. Employees should be able to access platforms, perform tasks, and make decisions from smartphones or tablets—whether they’re in the office, on the road, or working remotely. Mobile access ensures continuity and responsiveness in a fast-paced business environment.
  4. Centralization on a global scale
    To operate globally, businesses must embrace centralization through synchronized databases, platforms, reporting portals, and live dashboards. This allows for unified management and oversight, eliminating time zones and physical location as barriers to decision-making and execution.
  5. AI integration
    Finally, organizations should leverage AI and modern technologies across every aspect of the business. From predictive analytics in financial planning to intelligent automation in supply chains and personalized customer experiences, AI enhances agility, insight, and innovation. Integrating these technologies into workflows, security protocols, and strategic planning ensures that digitalization delivers long-term value.

Measuring success: digitalization metrics and maturity models

Successfully transitioning from digitization to digitalization demands continuous measurement and evaluation. Organizations can track progress using digitalization metrics such as:

  • Process automation rates
  • System integration levels
  • Data accessibility
  • User adoption
  • Time-to-decision improvements

These indicators help assess how effectively digital tools are transforming operations and delivering value.

To gain a broader view, many enterprises use digital maturity models. These frameworks evaluate an organization’s digital capabilities across dimensions like:

  • Strategy
  • Culture
  • Technology
  • Customer experience

Maturity levels typically range from basic digitization, such as digital records and isolated tools, to advanced digitalization, including AI-driven decision-making and fully integrated platforms. By benchmarking against these models, businesses can identify gaps, prioritize investments, and align digital initiatives with long-term goals.

Digitalization and the broader digital transformation journey

Digitalization is a critical phase within the larger arc of digital transformation, using data converted via digitization to enhance and automate business processes. Digital transformation encompasses a more holistic reinvention of how an organization operates, competes, and delivers value in a digital world.

True digital transformation involves rethinking business models, customer engagement strategies, and organizational culture. It’s not just about adopting new technologies—it’s about aligning those technologies with strategic goals, fostering innovation, and building resilience.

Digitalization serves as the bridge between foundational digitization and full-scale transformation, enabling businesses to move from simply having digital tools to using them in ways that fundamentally reshape operations and outcomes.

By understanding where digitalization fits within this journey, organizations can better assess their current maturity, identify gaps, and chart a path forward that leads to sustained growth, agility, and relevance in an increasingly digital economy.

FAQs

  • What is digital enablement?

    Digital enablement refers to equipping an organization with the tools, technologies, and capabilities needed to operate effectively in a digital environment. It involves preparing systems, processes, and people to adopt and benefit from digital solutions.

  • What is digital technology?

    Digital technology encompasses electronic tools, systems, devices, and resources that generate, store, or process data. Examples include cloud computing, artificial intelligence, mobile applications, and IoT devices—all of which support digital transformation.

  • Are there disadvantages to digitalization?

    Yes, digitalization can introduce challenges such as cybersecurity risks, high implementation costs, and resistance to change. Without proper planning and training, organizations may struggle with integration or fail to realize the full value of their digital investments.

  • What is the ROI of digitalization?

    The return on investment (ROI) from digitalization varies by industry and implementation, but it often includes increased efficiency, reduced operational costs, improved customer experiences, and faster decision-making. Long-term ROI also comes from enhanced agility and innovation.

  • When is digitization enough vs. when do you need to digitalize?

    Digitization is sufficient when the goal is to preserve or access information more efficiently—such as scanning documents or converting records. Digitalization becomes necessary when an organization wants to transform its operations, utilizing digital tools to automate, optimize, and innovate across processes.
