Ethics and compliance at SAP

By doing business the right way, SAP can positively impact social and economic development, furthering education, justice, democracy, prosperity, development, and health worldwide.

SAP is committed to the highest standards of ethical business. We strive to undertake business with integrity by doing the right thing and following both the spirit and the letter of the law in all the markets we operate in.

Teaching prevention

SAP has a strong culture of ethical business. We actively and continuously nurture our culture of integrity, providing our employees, partners and suppliers with clear and comprehensive guidance on our ethics and compliance standards via our Codes of Conduct, policies, and due diligence processes, and continuous advice, communication, and training.

Identifying concerns

At SAP, every employee has equal responsibility for upholding our standards of ethical business and is encouraged to voice their concerns about any situation, without fearing personal retribution. We equally encourage those who do business with us to speak out. Reports about any suspected issues can be made confidentially through our network of Field and People Compliance Officers, or anonymously – if desired – using our reporting tool, Speak Out at SAP.*

Addressing issues

We share reported concerns only with the relevant SAP department for assessment and next steps. For instance, matters of compliance relating to potential bribery, corruption, or fraud are investigated by a dedicated team within our Office of Ethics & Compliance. This team enacts appropriate measures to address any parties – employees, partners, or suppliers – found to have breached our Codes of Conduct. We also actively integrate learning from audits, investigations, data analytics, and industry best practices to continuously strengthen the elements of our compliance management system.

We rely on our employees and our ecosystem to assess every decision and action made on behalf of the organization to be legal and compliant with our Codes of Conduct and policies - as well as respectful towards people and the environment.

SAP encourages you to speak out and report any breaches of the Codes, including concerns over potential violations relating to compliance, the law and/or regulations.

There are multiple ways of raising a concern, including a direct approach to the responsible department, for example the Office of Ethics & Compliance, or the Global People Compliance Office, or by using the Speak Out at SAP tool. All submissions and identities are maintained confidentially to the greatest extent practical (in light of the need to take further steps).

There are also third-party channels for reporting potential misconduct that involves breaches of EU laws and national implementations thereof according to the EU Directive 2019/1937 on the protection of whistleblowers.

SAP has a strict non-retaliation policy and does not tolerate any threatened or actual retaliation toward individuals speaking up and reporting concerns about potential non-compliance in good faith.

If you believe you have been or are being subjected to retaliation for raising a concern, please use via Speak Out at SAP.

Note that anyone reporting a concern using the Speak Out tool is subject to the protections granted to them by the European Union Directive 2019/1937 on the protection of persons who report breaches of Union law (EU Whistleblower Directive), in as far as the requirements of the Directive and/or of national implementations thereof apply.

  • Provide a safe and professional work environment that protects SAP's intellectual property

SAP has been serious about climate action for more than 15 years, setting its first carbon reduction targets in 2009. SAP is committed to achieving net-zero emissions along its entire value chain in line with a 1.5°C future in 2030 - 20 years earlier than originally targeted.

 

In the transition to net zero 2030, SAP follows a three-pillar carbon mitigation hierarchy: avoid, reduce, contribute. This means avoiding the creation of greenhouse gases in the first place, for example by using virtual collaboration technology instead of business travel. Secondly, SAP aims to significantly reduce its unavoidable emissions through innovations, for example by increasing the energy efficiency of our offices and data centers or transitioning to an emission-free car fleet. SAP has put in place a portfolio of measures to continuously reduce emissions along the value chain. Thirdly, SAP is committed to contribute to climate change mitigation by financing climate solutions outside our value chain that equal in size the annual carbon footprint of our own operations until 2030. At net-zero, SAP will neutralize its remaining value chain emissions via carbon removals. 

SAP is committed to protecting the environment and continuously improving our own environmental performance. Therefore, we are gradually introducing an EMS reflecting the ISO 14001 standard at SAP sites worldwide. We aim to increase the EMS scope to 100% of SAP’s major company-owned sites by 2025 (2022: 85%). In addition, an ISO 50001-certified energy management system is integrated with existing management systems at selected sites, such as SAP’s headquarters. View our ISO certificates on sap.com (go to “Energy and environmental management”).

In alignment with SAP’s Global Environmental Policy, we remain committed to Zero Waste by striving for zero e-waste in our operations and diverting at least 90% of our discarded SAP equipment from incineration and landfill by 2030. By the end of 2025, 96% of our total discarded equipment had been reused or recycled. In addition, we are committed to embedding circularity principles across our operations and improving SAP’s overall waste performance. To advance our global goals, we continuously collaborate with suppliers, service providers, partners, our internal global network of sustainability champions, and other stakeholders.
