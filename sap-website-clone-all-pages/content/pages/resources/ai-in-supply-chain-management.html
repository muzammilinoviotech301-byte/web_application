What is AI in supply chain management?

AI in supply chain management helps optimize processes—from planning to manufacturing, logistics, and asset management—and improve decision-making.

Published on November 12, 202411 minutes

AI in supply chain management overview

Businesses are increasingly using AI to optimize the flow of goods—from the sourcing of raw materials to manufacturing and delivery—to help them operate more efficiently. Supply chains are complex, and managing them requires significant time and effort from different teams within a business, including procurement, QA, and production. But with the increasing availability of AI-enabled supply chain management solutions, businesses of all sizes now have access to transformative tools to both improve their processes and gain deeper insights into their supply chains data.

AI in supply chain management use cases

Businesses are using AI in supply chain management in different ways. Here are just a few examples:

  • Automating and monitoring the many individual tasks and communications necessary to move resources between the different links of the supply chain. For instance, digital assistants or AI copilots can facilitate routine communication by automatically responding to supplier inquiries, confirming orders, and updating delivery statuses to effectively streamline communication and reduce process delays.
  • Using machine learning algorithms to analyze vast amounts of data from various sources in real-time, identifying patterns and anomalies that could indicate potential delays or bottlenecks.
  • Streamlining supply chain operations by automating purchase order creation and management, monitoring shipment progress, notifying impacted parties when potential issues arise, and dynamically adjusting inventory levels.

How does AI work in supply chain management?

A wide range of AI technologies are used in supply chain management, including process automation, optimization algorithms, data-driven machine learning models, and generative AI. While some AI applications are trained on extensive datasets from various supply chain stages, others use predefined rules or mathematical models. Once implemented, these systems can analyze patterns, optimize processes, and provide insights to enhance decision-making.

Before we get into the specific mechanisms and examples of AI in supply chain management, let’s take a moment to understand some of the different types of data that supply chain management AI systems often work with:

  • Inventory data: Real-time stock levels, reorder points, and warehouse locations
  • Supplier performance data: Supplier reliability records, delivery times, and quality issues
  • Logistics and transportation data: Shipping routes, fuel usage, delivery times, and transportation costs
  • Customer demand data: Customer orders, returns, preferences, and seasonal trends
  • Weather and traffic data: External data like weather conditions and traffic patterns
  • Production and machinery data: Uptime, maintenance schedules, and equipment performance
  • Supplier cost data: Costs for raw materials, shipping, and labor
  • IoT sensor data: Temperature, humidity, or equipment status from Internet of Things (IoT) devices in warehouses, trucks, and factories
  • Market and economic data: Macroeconomic indicators such as commodity prices and market trends
  • Regulatory and compliance data: Data related to trade regulations, environmental compliance, and safety standards

The diversity and volume of this data can be massive, but many businesses are using specialized AI solutions to help them analyze it more effectively. This provides a bigger-picture, holistic view of what’s going on in a business’s supply chain than is possible (or affordable) using non-AI systems alone.

AI in supply chain management examples

Making mining operations more efficient

AI is being used to transform the early stages of the mining industry's supply chain by enhancing both efficiency and reliability. By analyzing sensor data from critical equipment like trucks and drills, AI can learn from historical data to predict potential equipment failures, enabling maintenance teams to intervene before breakdowns occur. And AI optimizes the routes of autonomous haulage systems (AHS) in real-time, ensuring that trucks follow the most efficient paths and conserve fuel.

Optimizing warehouse management

Businesses are significantly enhancing warehouse inventory management with help from AI. By analyzing vast amounts of data on customer orders, inventory levels, and product movement, AI systems accurately forecast demand and ensure optimal stock levels. Additionally, AI-driven insights help companies reorganize warehouse layouts to maximize space efficiency and reduce retrieval times, ultimately speeding up order fulfillment and improving overall operational efficiency.

Optimizing logistics

Logistics companies are using AI-enabled systems to optimize delivery routes. These systems analyze data such as package information, delivery locations, traffic patterns, and weather conditions to identify the most efficient routes in real time. This technology saves millions of miles of driving annually, reducing fuel consumption and operational costs while improving the reliability and predictability of delivery times.

10 benefits of AI in supply chain management

Implementing AI technologies in supply chain management offers a variety of benefits that enhance efficiency, reduce costs, and improve overall effectiveness. But there are also benefits to risk management, compliance, and other less obvious supply chain aspects which can have major impacts on businesses’ bottom lines and ability to be competitive.

Benefits of implementing AI technologies in supply chain management are:

  1. Enhanced efficiency: Automating routine, repetitive tasks reduces the time and effort required to manage supply chain operations, allowing human resources to focus on more strategic activities
  2. Improved equipment reliability: Predictive maintenance solutions alert teams when machinery needs servicing and can even dynamically adjust production schedules to reflect equipment downtime to keep operations running smoothly
  3. Smarter decision-making: Real-time insights and predictive analytics empower businesses to make informed decisions quickly and effectively in response to changing market conditions and world events
  4. Better accuracy: Automating error-prone manual data entry and providing deeper insights to aid in demand forecasting are just two of many ways AI can be used to eliminate errors and enhance human decision-making
  5. Facilities and workforce efficiency: Technologies like digital twins make it possible for businesses to optimize facilities layouts using 3D virtual modeling, and generative AI in supply chain—such as copilots—can help workers find the resources they need faster to do their jobs
  6. Supplier management: Using AI to analyze supplier performance metrics, conduct price comparisons, and provide recommendations for vendor selection helps businesses secure favorable terms and streamline procurement processes, reducing time and costs
  7. Risk management: Predicting and identifying potential risks, such as supplier failures or market fluctuations, allows businesses to develop contingency plans and strengthen supply chain resilience
  8. Scalability: Cloud-based AI computing services scale up or down as businesses need them, allowing them to manage larger volumes of data and more complex supply chain networks on demand
  9. Sustainability compliance: Monitoring the environmental impact of their supply chain—and taking steps to reduce impact—helps business achieve their sustainability goals, meet regulatory requirements, and be good corporate citizens
  10. End-to-end visibility: AI-enhanced tracking and transparency across the entire supply chain can help detect delays and bottlenecks early, allowing businesses to take quick corrective action

5 challenges of AI in supply chain management

Implementing AI in supply chain management is not without challenges, but they’re not insurmountable with research and planning:

  1. Data challenges: Inaccurate or irrelevant data can hinder AI models from providing reliable insights and recommendations, making it difficult to derive meaningful and actionable insights
  2. Capability and applicability: Not fully understanding AI capabilities and their applicability can complicate the process of identifying, scoping, prioritizing, and assessing the cost/benefit of AI use cases
  3. Regulations: New and evolving regulations aimed at addressing ethical implications of AI, privacy, and security concerns require companies to implement clear guidelines for the responsible use of AI
  4. Organizational readiness: Many organizations still operate with legacy and on-premises systems that may not integrate instantly with generative AI solutions, posing interoperability challenges and introducing security considerations for protecting sensitive data
  5. Human element: Resistance to change can slow down AI adoption; businesses must both convince teams to embrace new tools while also ensuring there’s a "human in the loop" to ensure that AI systems always have human oversight

AI in supply chain management for different industries

The specific role AI solutions play, and the benefits they deliver, differ across industries. Here are a few industry-specific examples:

Retail: Tracking sales trends and predicting demand, preventing overstock or shortages of popular items like clothing, electronics, and groceries. Retailers also use AI to streamline procurement by assisting in negotiations with suppliers.

Food and beverage: Helping manage perishable goods by analyzing storage conditions and optimizing delivery routes. Businesses use AI to predict demand for products like dairy or produce, making sure they are replenished just in time, reducing waste.

E-commerce: Improving order fulfillment by optimizing and automating warehouse operations to enable faster deliveries and fewer errors. Companies like Amazon rely on AI to manage warehouse robots and delivery fleets, boosting efficiency.

Automotive: Streamlining production in the automotive industry by automating supply orders and managing global supplier networks. The use of AI enables plants to have the right components, like tires and engines, without holding excess inventory.

Healthcare: Enabling pharmaceutical companies to track the flow of medicines and medical devices, predicting demand for critical supplies. This helps hospitals and pharmacies stay stocked with life-saving products like vaccines and surgical tools.

Fashion: Predicting style trends and optimizing supply chains to keep up with seasonal demand. Businesses use AI to manage everything from fabric sourcing to distribution so that the latest collections hit retail stores and online platforms on time.

How AI in supply chain management can enhance sustainability

AI is proving to be a useful tool to help businesses make supply chains more sustainable by optimizing processes, reducing waste, and improving resource efficiency. Perhaps the most familiar example is AI trip planning to optimize transportation routes based on traffic, weather, and mapping data to help reduce fuel consumption. And predictive analytics AI tools don’t just help retailers stock the right goods in the right places and at the right time, they also serve to reduce overproduction, minimize excess inventory, and prevent unnecessary waste—all key factors in supply chain sustainability.

In supplier management, AI tools can help businesses identify suppliers that follow sustainable practices, ensuring responsible sourcing of materials. By monitoring the environmental impact of suppliers and flagging deviations from sustainability targets, companies can maintain ethical, sustainable partnerships throughout their supply chain.

AI for supply chain management solutions: how to prepare

Implementing AI supply chain management solutions takes careful planning. The first step is auditing current processes to see where AI could add the most value. For example, it’s important to understand where bottlenecks or inefficiencies already exist that might be relatively easy to remediate with AI. After identifying these areas, businesses can start laying the groundwork for more comprehensive AI solutions—including determining if it makes sense for them to build their own solution or use off-the-shelf, ready-to-use options.

Next, businesses should clean up their data. AI relies on high-quality, accurate data to function properly, so it’s essential to collect the right data and make it accessible to the AI applications that will use it. Training employees is also critical because workers need to understand how AI tools function and how to use them to enhance their productivity.

Perhaps the most important part of preparing your supply chain for AI is having a clear strategy and roadmap. It may not be practical to implement multiple AI solutions all at once, so having a plan that outlines the order of steps, along with a realistic timeline, will make for a smoother implementation.

AI preparation checklist

Audit current processes

  • Identify inefficiencies, bottlenecks, or repetitive tasks: Review key supply chain areas (inventory, logistics, production) to spot slowdowns, errors, or labor-intensive tasks. Prioritize processes that are time-consuming or prone to human error
  • Assess which areas could benefit most from AI solutions: Focus on high-impact areas like demand forecasting, inventory management, and logistics optimization where AI enhances speed, accuracy, and cost savings

Evaluate data readiness

  • Confirm that your data is clean, accurate, and up-to-date: Conduct regular audits of your data to remove duplication, correct errors, and ensure consistency across platforms
  • Organize and store data in a multimodal database: Different forms of AI consume different types of data, so it’s helpful to organize and store data in a multimodal database within a single system to meet these various needs

Set measurable goals

  • Define clear objectives for AI implementation: Set specific targets like reducing order errors by 20% or cutting delivery times by 15%—tie these goals to supply chain KPIs
  • Align AI goals with broader business strategies: Ensure AI initiatives support overall business objectives, like improving customer satisfaction or reducing environmental impact

Invest in the right tools and technology

  • Determine if pre-built or custom-built solutions are right for you: There’s an array of pre-built AI solutions for supply chain use cases on the market, so assess if any will meet your needs before investing in a custom solution
  • Select AI platforms or tools that align with your supply chain needs: Consider tools like predictive analytics for demand forecasting, machine learning for route optimization, and computer vision for quality control
  • Verify that systems can scale as your AI needs grow: Choose cloud-based platforms that can handle increasing data loads and expand as your supply chain grows in complexity

Train your workforce

  • Provide training on AI tools and their integration into daily workflows: Offer hands-on training for staff to understand AI interfaces and their roles in managing the systems
  • Encourage collaboration between AI systems and human expertise: Promote a culture where employees use AI insights to enhance their decision-making rather than replacing their human judgment

Monitor and adjust

  • Continuously evaluate AI performance and adjust: Regularly track AI's impact on key metrics (such as cost savings, speed) and tweak algorithms as needed
  • Use data-driven insights to refine AI strategies and maximize results: Leverage real-time analytics to continually optimize AI systems and uncover new opportunities for improvement
