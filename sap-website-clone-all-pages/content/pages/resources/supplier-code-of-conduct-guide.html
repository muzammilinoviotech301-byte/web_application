What is a supplier code of conduct

A supplier code of conduct defines the social, environmental, and ethical expectations for supplier behavior. It helps organizations improve traceability, reduce risk, and support regulatory compliance—while fostering responsible, resilient supply chains.

Supplier code of conduct meaning

A supplier code of conduct is a shared agreement between a company and its suppliers that outlines expected behaviors across areas like labor rights, environmental practices, and ethical business conduct. It helps ensure that suppliers meet consistent standards—supporting safer workplaces, fair treatment of employees, and responsible sourcing across the supply chain.

For global organizations, a supplier code of conduct plays a critical role in aligning operations with broader sustainability and compliance goals. It also builds confidence with customers, partners, and regulators by demonstrating a commitment to doing business the right way.

Why having a supplier code matters

Strong supply chains depend on more than logistics. They require trust, transparency, and shared accountability. A supplier code of conduct helps companies promote ethical behavior and reduce risk by setting clear expectations for partners—wherever they operate.

By defining these standards, businesses can protect their brand reputation, improve supplier relationships, and contribute to a more sustainable and resilient supply network. It’s also an important tool in meeting regulatory obligations, such as those related to due diligence and traceability.

How to create a supplier code of conduct

The multi-step process for creating a supplier code of conduct

Creating a supplier code of conduct begins with defining the values and standards that are most important to your organization—and translating those into clear, actionable expectations for suppliers. Here’s a practical approach to guide the process:

  • Start with your priorities
    Identify core values and operational standards that reflect your business goals, regulatory obligations, and industry norms.
  • Benchmark best practices
    Review codes of conduct from industry leaders and peers to understand common frameworks and identify gaps.
  • Tailor to your supply chain
    Customize your code to address specific risks and opportunities relevant to your supplier ecosystem.
  • Engage internal stakeholders
    Collaborate with teams across procurement, legal, sustainability, and compliance to align content and implementation.
  • Make it accessible
    Ensure the code is written in clear language, translated as needed, and easy for suppliers to reference.
  • Support with training
    Develop communication and onboarding materials to reinforce expectations and drive adoption over time.

Key elements to include in a supplier code of conduct

An effective supplier code of conduct covers a core set of principles that reflect your company’s ethical, environmental, and operational standards. While the specifics may vary by industry or geography, most codes include the following key elements:

  • Labor and human rights: Expectations for fair wages, working hours, non-discrimination, and the prohibition of forced or child labor.
  • Health and safety: Requirements for safe working conditions, proper training, and the prevention of workplace hazards.
  • Environmental responsibility: Standards for managing emissions, waste, and resource use in line with global sustainability goals
  • Business integrity: Clear policies on anti-corruption, data protection, and transparent financial practices.
  • Compliance and documentation: Guidance on record-keeping, auditing rights, and cooperation with relevant inspections or certifications.

These components create a foundation for long-term supplier partnerships built on mutual accountability and shared values.

Supplier code of conduct examples

Supplier codes of conduct can vary depending on industry, region, and company priorities—but many follow a similar framework. Looking at examples from well-established organizations can provide a helpful starting point for building or refining your own.

SAP’s own Supplier Code of Conduct outlines clear standards for ethical conduct, sustainability, and compliance, offering a model for how companies can formalize their expectations across diverse supplier networks.

How to enforce your supplier code

Creating a supplier code of conduct is just the first step. Ensuring it’s followed requires a consistent approach to communication, monitoring, and accountability. Start by embedding the code into supplier onboarding processes, contracts, and regular touchpoints. Make expectations clear from the outset and ensure suppliers understand their responsibilities.

Regular assessments—such as audits, questionnaires, or third-party verifications—can help track compliance. In some cases, digital tools can enhance traceability by validating sustainability data and automatically managing reporting requirements, particularly when regulations like the EUDR come into play.

When noncompliance is detected, it’s important to respond constructively. Provide opportunities for remediation where possible but also be prepared to escalate or exit relationships that pose significant risks.

How a supplier code of conduct contributes to a sustainable supply chain

A supplier code of conduct isn’t just about compliance—it’s a foundational element of a sustainable supply chain. By setting clear expectations for ethical conduct and environmental responsibility, businesses can foster greater transparency and accountability across their supplier networks.

This visibility supports more informed decision-making, strengthens resilience, and helps reduce the environmental and social risks that can disrupt operations. It also empowers companies to align purchasing decisions with their sustainability goals and customer expectations—without compromising quality or efficiency.

When combined with supply chain compliance tools and integrated data systems, a supplier code becomes a catalyst for broader transformation—enabling organizations to deliver responsibly sourced, compliant products with confidence.
