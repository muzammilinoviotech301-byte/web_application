How to build an effective AI strategy

Gain practical insights and learn about the 8 AI strategy framework steps to use the full potential of AI in your business.

Published on August 7, 2025

Understand AI strategy

Successful AI adoption goes far beyond just acquiring technology. It requires a well-crafted AI strategy that aligns with a business’s goals, fosters innovation, and drives measurable outcomes.

Let’s explore why an AI strategy is critical, the elements it should include, and how to overcome challenges during implementation.

Why an AI strategy is crucial for success

Launching AI projects without a clear strategy can lead to wasted resources, low adoption, and missed opportunities. An AI strategy provides a road map that guides an organization. It helps business leaders identify where to invest, prioritize initiatives, and seamlessly integrate AI into existing processes.

Without this strategic framework, businesses risk disjointed efforts, fragmented data, and misaligned objectives. This makes it difficult to scale AI initiatives or realize their full potential. Alternatively, organizations with a robust AI strategy enjoy better project success rates, improved operational efficiency, and competitive advantage.

Cultivating an innovative culture

A thriving AI strategy starts with fostering an innovative culture. Organizations must encourage experimentation, tolerate calculated and data-backed risks, and empower cross-functional teams to explore AI’s possibilities. This culture fuels creativity, drives continuous learning, and creates momentum around AI adoption.

Leaders play a vital role here. They should promote transparency, recognize AI power users, and encourage open communication channels across departments. When innovation is embedded into the company’s DNA, AI initiatives can evolve beyond pilot phases into scalable, impactful solutions.

Eight steps for developing an AI strategy framework

To build a successful AI strategy, organizations need a structured framework that includes a clear vision, measurable goals, governance, and a well-defined operating model. This foundation, if set up correctly, can help ensure alignment and AI adoption across teams.

By following the following eight steps, you'll be better equipped to use the full potential of AI:

1. Determine AI vision and approach

Business leaders should begin by defining their AI vision: the role AI will play within the organization. Whether the goal is to enhance customer experience, automate manual tasks, improve decision-making, or develop new products, a clear vision sets the tone and aligns stakeholders around a shared purpose.

Next, they must determine the organization's AI approach. Will the company build capabilities in-house, partner with external vendors, or use cloud-based AI platforms? This approach should be guided by available resources, the desired timeline, and the complexity of the AI solutions. Having an AI strategy enables businesses to anticipate opportunities and challenges rather than merely respond to trends or problems as they arise. This proactive approach helps companies stay ahead of competitors and better use AI’s potential for long-term growth.

2. Set SMART goals

It’s important for organizations to set SMART—specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound—goals for their AI initiatives. Leaders should ask:

  • What processes should be optimized or automated?
  • Which outcomes need improvement (for example, cost reduction, revenue growth, or enhanced customer satisfaction)?
  • How will AI contribute to overarching business objectives?

By aligning AI projects with business goals, organizations can make sure that their efforts are focused, strategic, and deliver meaningful value.

3. Explore continuing education

Courses and webinars are invaluable when learning about AI. SAP offers AI-focused resources that guide businesses through identifying use cases, assessing readiness, and understanding potential impact. Check out free webcasts, courses, and certification opportunities.

4. Estimate and forecast ROI

Quantifying AI’s value can be challenging, but it’s necessary to justify investments.

Estimating ROI involves understanding the full lifecycle costs—such as development, integration, and maintenance—and weighing them against the expected benefits. Pricing often works by charging for usage in units. So, for example, a business needs to understand how many units they’re likely to consume to accurately estimate costs and calculate ROI. Establishing accurate forecasting can inform budget allocation and get buy-in from executives and stakeholders.

5. Create key performance indicators (KPIs)

KPIs that are aligned with an organization's AI goals help enable ongoing measurement and course correction. Relevant KPIs may include:

  • Accuracy and speed of AI models
  • Reduction in processing times
  • Customer satisfaction scores
  • Operational cost savings
  • Increase in revenue attributable to AI

For example, a KPI might be invoice processing time, and the target could be cutting processing time by 40% within a year through AI automation. By tracking these metrics, organizations can assess whether AI projects meet expectations and where improvements are needed.

6. Define AI technology and data strategy

A robust AI strategy must incorporate a comprehensive data strategy. AI’s effectiveness hinges on access to high-quality, relevant data. This means an organization must understand their current data landscape, address gaps, and make sure data governance standards are in place.

Organizations should consider:

  • What data sources are available internally and externally?
  • How will data be collected, stored, and secured?
  • What technologies support data integration and AI model deployment?

Clear articulation of the technology stack and data processes supports scalable AI development.

7. Establish AI governance

Governance frameworks ensure AI is developed responsibly and ethically. They address issues such as data privacy, model transparency, bias mitigation, and compliance with regulations.

Establishing AI governance helps build trust with customers, employees, and regulators. It also protects the organization from reputational and legal risks associated with AI misuse or errors.

8. Choose an operating model and resources

An organization’s AI operating model defines how AI initiatives are executed and supported. It includes team structure, methods of collaboration, resource allocation, and decision-making processes.

Key considerations include:

  • Centralized vs. decentralized AI teams
  • Roles and responsibilities (including the AI strategist)
  • Processes for model development, deployment, and monitoring
  • Training and upskilling programs for employees

An effective operating model balances agility with control to deliver AI value consistently.

AI strategy best practices

A successful AI strategy requires more than just adopting new technologies—it demands a thoughtful, structured approach that aligns with the organization's overall vision and goals. To drive real value from AI, businesses must focus on several key components to ensure AI initiatives are not only innovative but also address long-term business priorities:

  • Define clear goals. Clear, aligned goals are the foundation of any AI strategy. Goals help teams prioritize, allocate resources, and measure success.
  • Appoint an AI strategist. This role acts as a bridge between technical teams and business leaders. This person translates business objectives into AI use cases, manages stakeholder expectations, and ensures initiatives align with the broader strategy.
  • Build the right team. Successful AI teams blend diverse skills, such as data science, engineering, domain expertise, project management, and change management. The right mix—businesses can pull people from existing teams within the organization to get started—enables innovative solutions that address real needs.

Overcome challenges during an AI strategy rollout

Implementing an AI strategy can present a variety of challenges. Organizations must navigate these obstacles carefully to realize the full potential of AI:

  • Issues with data. Data quality and availability often pose hurdles. Incomplete, siloed, or unstructured data can delay AI development. Addressing these issues early through data governance and integration efforts is crucial.
  • AI governance challenges. Establishing governance frameworks requires balancing innovation with risk management. While this process might hinder progress, these frameworks are essential to ensure responsible AI use.
  • Buy-in and alignment. AI initiatives span multiple departments. Without alignment, projects may face internal resistance or conflicting priorities. Engaging stakeholders early—through workshops, clear communication, and demonstrating quick wins—helps build consensus.

Understanding common pitfalls and adopting proactive solutions is essential for business leaders to achieve a smooth rollout.

Conclusion

A clear AI strategy lays the foundation for effective adoption and sustained innovation. It aligns technology investments with business goals, fosters an innovative culture, ensures data and governance readiness, and sets measurable targets for performance. By investing time and resources in developing a strategic AI framework, organizations can discover AI’s full potential—turning cutting-edge technology into a sustainable, competitive advantage.
