AI won’t transform your ERP. Until you rewire it.

Most companies bolt AI onto old ERP processes. That’s not transformation. Here’s the mindset shift that moves the needle.

Companies everywhere are rushing to add AI to their ERP systems. The demos look impressive. The use case catalogs are long and colorful. But the results? For most organizations, they’re disappointing.

According to MIT’s Project NANDA, 95% of enterprise generative AI pilots deliver no measurable P&L impact. The gap between AI ambition and AI results isn’t a technology problem. It’s a process problem.

We recently sat down with Sharonee Dutta, Managing Director at BCG Platinion and CTO of ERP, and Koushik Ray, Partner and Director at Boston Consulting Group (BCG), on the Future of ERP podcast. Here are some of the takeaways from our conversation.

Why adding AI on top of ERP doesn’t work

Sharonee framed it perfectly: you can put AI on top of your existing ERP the same way you can put a powerful engine onto a fairly terrible car. You’ll go faster, but you may not go somewhere better.

When AI is layered onto an unchanged process, what you actually get is:

  • Faster task completion of tasks that may not need to exist
  • Better reporting on a process built for a different era
  • Marginal productivity gains, but no real operating model change

The deeper question isn’t "How do I make this process faster?" It’s: "If AI were part of my workforce from day one, would I have designed this process at all?"

Take finance as an example. You can add intelligent reporting on top of your existing close process. Or you can redesign the entire end-to-end close so that AI agents handle coordination, analysis, and follow-up, while humans focus on judgment and exceptions.

Those are two completely different value propositions. One is optimization. The other is transformation.

The three mistakes companies keep making

Most organizations get stuck in what Sharonee calls the "AI petting zoo," with lots of interesting pilots everyone gets to visit, but very little actually makes it into the P&L. Here are the failure patterns she sees most often:

Mistake 1: Too many priorities

If you have 73 AI use cases, you don’t have 73 priorities. You have a prioritization problem. Resources get diluted, decisions get deferred, and nothing moves. A 2025 S&P Global study found that 42% of companies abandoned most of their AI initiatives that year, up from just 17% the year before.

Mistake 2: Treating AI as a technology project

You can have the most impressive models, the most beautiful architecture, and the most polished demos, but if no one changed the process, the roles, or the way decisions get made, you’ve run a very expensive experiment.

Mistake 3: Automating what shouldn't exist

AI applied to a bad process gives you a faster bad process. Before automating anything, ask the uncomfortable question: if you were starting over today, would this process even exist in this form?

What do companies getting it right actually do?

The best companies Sharonee works with do three things differently.

  1. They start with the business problem, not the technology.
    AI is moving so fast it’s easy to spend six to twelve months admiring the tools and forgetting why you bought them. The real value isn’t in the algorithm. It’s in changing the function, the process, the roles, and the operating model.
  2. They go deep on two or three use cases—not wide on seventy.
    The winning companies pick two or three opportunities that can materially move a KPI the CEO or CFO actually cares about, then go deep. Not broad, not comprehensive. Deep. According to Protiviti’s 2025 AI Meets ERP survey, 64% of organizations classify AI-enabled ERP transformation as a critical or high strategic priority. The discipline to focus is what separates those generating real value from everyone else.
  3. They redesign the function end-to-end.
    In manufacturing, this looks like AI agents working across sales, engineering, and production planning simultaneously—analyzing a tender, supporting product design, planning industrialization across the network. Humans shift toward supervision and exception management. That’s not a chatbot next to an old process. That’s a redesigned operating model.

    A concrete example: a mid-market industrial company with under £2 billion in revenue focused on just three areas—maintenance optimization, AI-enabled procurement, and capital project control. BCG estimated approximately £55 million in annual value from those three areas alone, with a clear pathway to nearly double it.

    The discipline of getting there matters as much as the number. Start by asking where value is leaking. Then identify which decisions drive that value. Then ask how AI can materially change those decisions.

What does AI-first ERP delivery actually look like?

Beyond strategy, AI is also changing how ERP programs get delivered. Koushik Ray has spent two decades running large-scale ERP transformations. He describes a three-part shift in how the work itself gets done.

  1. A governed context layer: This ingests real process flows, architecture, custom code, and configuration. What Koushik calls ‘the company memory.’ Not workshop notes. Not belief audits. Actual system evidence that becomes the ground truth for all AI reasoning across the program.
  2. Orchestrated AI agents: A solution confirmation agent, a functional spec drafter, and a test automation agent, working across the program in sequence under quality gates. Humans review only the high-stakes items.
  3. The crucial mental flip: Humans move from producing artifacts to approving them. AI drafts the fit-gap analysis, the specs, the test scripts. Humans validate. That’s what AI-first delivery actually means in practice: not tools bolted onto the old model, but a completely new way of running a transformation program.

BCG estimates that this approach can reduce overall ERP implementation effort by 20–40%. Some specific phases (like test script generation) see even higher gains. But be cautious of any vendor or system integrator (SI) quoting isolated phase savings as if they applied to the entire program. The real prize isn’t finishing faster. It’s what you do with the time you save. If that savings helps you reach richer business outcomes sooner, that’s when AI-first delivery truly pays off.

Which parts of ERP are ready for AI—and which aren’t?

Not every ERP function will embrace AI at the same pace. That’s predictable, and understanding the pattern helps you sequence your strategy.

Take finance. It’s actually two very different worlds:

AI readiness

Why

Finance operations (close, compliance, audit)

Cautious, gradual adoption

Heavily rule-based by design. Compliance and traceability requirements are non-negotiable.

Financial planning and analysis (FP&A)

Strong appetite, faster adoption

Scenario modeling, variance analysis, and forecasting benefit enormously from AI handling complexity at scale.

This isn’t a binary choice between AI and rule-based processes. It’s about understanding which decisions benefit from AI reasoning, and sequencing accordingly.

Three myths about AI and ERP—Busted

Myth 1: You need perfect data before you can start.

You don’t. Perfect data is a wonderful destination, but no one actually lives there. Pick the problem. Identify which data matters. Start learning. Improve quality as you go. Waiting for perfect data is often just a reason to postpone.

Myth 2: Moving to cloud ERP is primarily a technology upgrade.

It’s not. If the business works exactly the same way after a cloud migration, you’ve given the company nicer plumbing. Maybe useful, but that’s not the transformation you wanted to put in front of the board. The technology is actually the easiest part. Changing how the business runs on top of it is the hard part.

Myth 3: Once you’ve implemented cloud ERP, you’re ready to scale AI.

Cloud ERP gives you a stronger foundation: modern architecture, a common transactional backbone, better data access. But it’s a foundation, not a house. Go-live is pouring the concrete. The interesting work begins after.

What's the future of ERP?

Here’s the most provocative take from the conversation: we’re going to stop talking about ERP.

Not because ERP stops mattering—it won’t. But because ERP will become so embedded in how a business operates that it stops being a "system people use" and becomes the operating fabric of the enterprise.

Your ERP platform will determine how quickly you can deploy AI, redesign processes, and launch new capabilities. That makes the choice of platform—and the operating model you build on top of it—a strategic decision, not just a technology one.

And from a delivery perspective, the concept of an ERP "go-live" will fade. There’s no single finish line. There’s a continuous journey—enabled by AI-first delivery models that reach milestones faster, maintain momentum, and keep transformation moving forward.

The window is open—but it won’t stay that way

The companies moving forward with clarity—focused use cases, process redesign, an AI-first delivery model—are already pulling ahead.

The question isn’t whether AI and ERP belong together. They clearly do. The question is whether you’re using one to do more of the same—or to build something fundamentally better. That answer is entirely within your control.
